# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-12

## 1. Governing AI Research Through Peer Review: A Mixed-Methods Study of the Longitudinal Effects of Ethics Flags Across Resubmissions [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Kento Nishi, Alec Laprevotte, Isaiah Bullock, Mfoniso Andrew
**Date:** September 9, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10740
**Summary:** The authors ask whether AI conferences' ethics flags, introduced to steer research toward safer and more responsible practices, actually change what researchers study or just how they present it. Tracking 446 ICLR submissions that were rejected or withdrawn with ethics flags into their later public resubmissions, they find that in 83% of cases authors left the flagged concern unaddressed or reworded the paper without altering the underlying methods, and interviews confirm authors treat peer review as an editorial filter over publication rather than a lever on research direction. The paper recommends disclosing prior ethics flags at resubmission so accountability carries over across venues, since current review-based governance largely fails to redirect the research it is meant to shape.

## 2. Wavering Oracles: Selective Updating and Correlated Failures in LLMs and Their Implications for Scientific Workflows [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Xiaoshn Nee, Haobo Zhong, Xiaomin Ni
**Date:** September 10, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11428
**Summary:** The authors probe whether large language models used as tools within scientific workflows preserve correct conclusions, accept valid corrections, and produce errors that a downstream selector can reliably catch. Testing ten models across 17,055 trajectories on a controlled benchmark, they find wide variation in this "selective updating" ability, from models that adapt correctly to suggestions to ones destabilized by them or unable to distinguish good advice from bad, and only modest correlation between models' errors, meaning ensembling several imperfect models still meaningfully improves reliability. A simple reliability selector recovers most of the gap between an average model and the theoretical best-of-ensemble ceiling, suggesting concrete design targets for making multi-model AI pipelines trustworthy in research settings.

## 3. AI Innovation and Firm Performance in the Medical Device Industry [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Fazliddin Shermatov, Stephane Robin, Aldo Geuna
**Date:** September 8, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08485
**Summary:** Using FDA device-clearance records as a rare directly observable measure of firm-level AI innovation, the authors link them to patents, publications, and financial data for medical technology firms and estimate a three-stage model tracing the path from external collaboration to AI device launch to firm performance. They find that external research partnerships, especially with industry and clinical partners rather than academic ones, are a robust driver of bringing AI devices to market, with disproportionately large effects for small firms with limited in-house R&D. Firms that launch AI devices see durable gains in labor productivity, though effects on profit margins are weaker and fade in some specifications, consistent with competition eroding pricing power as AI diffuses through the sector.
