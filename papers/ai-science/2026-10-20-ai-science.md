# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-10-20

## 1. AutoResearch: An Execution-Grounded Multi-Agent Framework for Reliable Research Workflow Automation [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Rajesh Kumar, Waqar Ali, Junaid Ahmed, Abdullah Aman Khan, Shaoning Zeng
**Date:** October 12, 2026 (v2; first posted May 4, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02520
**Summary:** The authors build AutoResearch, a multi-agent system that automates research workflows by running generated code in a sandbox, iteratively repairing failures, and auditing whether cited sources actually support the claims made. Rather than trusting agent output directly, the framework treats execution errors, failed citation checks, and reviewer-agent feedback as filters that catch unreliable research artifacts before they are used. Across coding benchmarks and small end-to-end research tasks, this grounding improved execution success and citation validity relative to comparable agent baselines, positioning the system as a reliability layer for AI-assisted research rather than an autonomous scientist.

## 2. How much does the map of science depend on the embedding model? [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Alejandro Treny Ortega
**Date:** October 12, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16639
**Summary:** The author compares ten text-embedding models used to build "maps of science" from the titles and abstracts of 500,000 publications spanning 26 disciplines, testing whether different models produce the same picture of how research relates. Broad, discipline-level arrangements stayed similar across models, but at finer grain the models disagreed sharply: two models shared only about a third of a given paper's 25 nearest neighbors on average, and even judgments about which discipline's research was more topically spread out could flip depending on the model and how its output was processed. The finding is a caution for AI-for-science tools that rely on embedding-based maps, since conclusions about local structure and cross-field comparisons can be artifacts of model choice rather than stable features of the literature.

## 3. Multiplex coupling: Connecting the intellectual base and the topics of a research field [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Massimo Aria, Corrado Cuccurullo, Maria Spano
**Date:** October 15, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21858
**Summary:** The authors propose "multiplex coupling," a bibliometric method that keeps citation-based similarity between papers (their intellectual base) and term-based similarity (their topics) as two separate but linked layers, instead of collapsing them into one map as prior hybrid approaches do. Consensus clustering on this structure identifies "roots" sharing citations and "themes" sharing topics, and tracking how roots branch into themes over time reveals cases where a single intellectual tradition gives rise to several distinct research directions. Tests on Web of Science, Scopus, and OpenAlex data show the two layers carry largely complementary information, offering researchers and funders a sharper tool for tracing how fields actually develop and diversify.

## 4. On some unresolved issues in aggregate level comparative research evaluation [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Paul Donner
**Date:** October 16, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23318
**Summary:** This conceptual paper revisits two persistent methodological problems in comparing countries' or institutions' research performance: how a chosen reference group distorts the resulting impact scores, and how international collaboration mechanically dilutes the accuracy of citation-based impact measures, pushing countries' apparent performance toward convergence. The author weighs existing fixes against newly proposed alternatives, without resolving which is best, arguing instead that the field needs renewed methodological engagement with these long-standing issues. For science policy, the paper is a reminder that cross-country rankings built on citation impact may partly reflect measurement artifacts rather than real differences in research performance.
