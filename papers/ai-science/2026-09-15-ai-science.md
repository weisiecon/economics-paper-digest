# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-15

## 1. Has Scientific Talent Shifted from Depth to Breadth? Evidence across Papers, Knowledge Inputs, Careers, and Teams [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Xiaoshn Nee, Haobo Zhong, Xiaomin Ni
**Date:** September 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14425
**Summary:** Drawing on 47,959 papers across six fields (2010-2025), 51,736 cited works, and career histories for 1,754 researchers, the authors test whether scientific work is shifting from individual specialization toward broader generalism as team science and AI tools spread. They find team sizes grew about 37% while individual papers' topic breadth narrowed slightly, cited knowledge stayed stable to modestly broader, established researchers' personal breadth crept up only marginally, and deeper individual focus continued to predict higher citation impact, with no clear shift tied to AI intensity after 2022. The results suggest depth and breadth are coexisting rather than trading off — individuals keep specializing while teams and citation networks supply the breadth — pushing back on narratives that generative AI is flattening scientific expertise.

## 2. Ending the NIH embargo accelerated public access to funded research, but substantial delays remain [Science Policy]

**Authors:** Haining Wang
**Date:** September 13, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14842
**Summary:** The author exploits NIH's July 2025 policy change requiring immediate public deposit of funded articles in PubMed Central (replacing the prior 12-month embargo) as a natural experiment, comparing NIH-backed articles to same-journal, same-month articles without federal support before and after the change. Availability within a week of publication rose 5.6 percentage points more for NIH-supported articles, with the gain concentrated in non-open-access journals (where one-week availability roughly doubled from 11% to 22%) and essentially no differential effect in journals already in the Directory of Open Access Journals. The policy worked as intended but only partially — even months after the embargo ended, most NIH-funded articles in non-open-access journals still weren't in PubMed Central within a week — showing that mandates speed up public access without guaranteeing near-immediate compliance.

## 3. From Funding to Findings (FIND): An Open Database of NSF Awards and Research Outputs [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Kazimier Smith, Yucheng Lu, Qiaochu Fan
**Date:** September 14, 2026 (v2; originally posted October 11, 2025)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10336
**Summary:** The authors build FIND, an open dataset linking NSF grant proposals to the publications and outputs they produced, aimed at supporting metascience research on how public funding translates into research output. As proof-of-concept applications, they show that the language of grant proposals carries some signal about the future citation impact of funded work, and use LLMs to extract explicit claims from proposals and later publications to measure how well funded projects deliver on what they promised. The dataset itself is the main contribution, offering a large-scale structured resource for studying returns to public science funding, informing funding policy, and enabling new AI-driven analyses of the research pipeline.

## 4. Geometric Signatures of Conceptual Reorganization: A Counterfactual Embedding Framework for Detecting Scientific Revolutions [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Dimitris Ntounis, Ariel Schwartzman, Chris Chafe, Thomas A. Ryckman
**Date:** September 14, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14917
**Summary:** The authors propose measuring "conceptual reorganization" in science by embedding scientific documents and observing how the geometry of that embedding space shifts when documents tied to a candidate concept are counterfactually removed, comparing before versus after the concept's historical emergence. Testing the approach on five landmark cases — special relativity, Gödel's incompleteness theorems, the Higgs mechanism, deep learning, and the transformer attention mechanism — they detect measurable geometric signatures of conceptual restructuring, while also surfacing limitations from sparse historical data and uncertain document-to-concept assignment. The work offers a quantitative, embedding-based alternative to qualitative historiography for identifying when fields undergo genuine conceptual shifts rather than incremental growth.

## 5. How Do We Engage with Other Disciplines? A Framework to Study Meaningful Interdisciplinary Discourse in Scholarly Publications [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Bagyasree Sudharsan, Alexandria Leto, Maria Leonor Pacheco
**Date:** September 13, 2026 (v3; originally posted January 15, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17020
**Summary:** Noting that existing metrics for interdisciplinary research (affiliation mixes, keywords, citation counts) don't capture whether a paper actually engages with ideas from other fields, the authors build a citation-purpose taxonomy designed for interdisciplinary work and validate it with an annotation study. They apply the framework to publications at the intersection of NLP and computational social science, showing it can quantify the depth and quality of cross-disciplinary citation engagement rather than just its presence. The tool gives funders, journals, and researchers a more precise way to evaluate whether "interdisciplinary" papers are substantively integrating outside fields or just citing them superficially, relevant as institutions increasingly reward interdisciplinary work.
