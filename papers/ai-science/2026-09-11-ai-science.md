# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-11

## 1. SurveyAgent-HKA: A multi-agent framework for scientific survey generation with LLMs and human knowledge augmentation [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Tong Bao, Mir Tafseer Nayeem, Yi Zhao, Davood Rafiei, Chengzhi Zhang
**Date:** September 5, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05938
**Summary:** The authors introduce SurveyAgent-HKA, a multi-agent LLM framework that generates scientific literature surveys by retrieving papers from multiple sources, clustering them into topics, and refining the resulting outline against human-written surveys before drafting. It further mines peer-review comments from published surveys to guide revisions, replicating the expert review cycle that one-pass LLM survey generators skip. Across two domains the framework beats existing baselines on citation quality, structural consistency, and content quality while staying efficient enough for practical use.

## 2. Mapping the Emerging Social Science of Large Language Models [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Yi Yang, Xiao Jia, Zeyun Dong, Chenzhang Wang, Zhanzhan Zhao
**Date:** September 7, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07598
**Summary:** Combining a hand-reviewed set of 198 papers with a field-scale corpus of nearly 48,000 publications, the authors use embeddings, clustering, and topic modeling to map the emerging social-science literature on large language models into three domains: model behavior, multi-agent "LLM societies," and human-LLM interaction. The taxonomy holds up well under resampling and largely matches authors' own classifications, but citation patterns diverge sharply by venue, with behavior- and society-level work concentrated in top conferences and human-interaction work concentrated in journals. The study offers a reproducible map of how this fast-growing research direction is organizing itself across subfields and publication venues.

## 3. Knowing Your Uncertainty -- On the application of LLM in social sciences [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Bolun Zhang, Linzhuo Li, Yunqi Chen, Qinlin Zhao, Zihan Zhu, Xiaoyuan Yi, Xing Xie
**Date:** December 5, 2025 (updated September 6, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05461
**Summary:** The authors argue that using LLMs as research instruments in computational social science requires the same explicit uncertainty quantification long expected of other empirical methods, given the models' opaque training and stochastic outputs. They convert existing uncertainty-quantification metrics onto a common scale built from Hill numbers, a family of diversity measures, so variation in LLM outputs can be compared across tasks and notions of similarity. Four empirical applications show how the framework can help researchers judge when LLM-generated evidence is reliable enough to use as social-science research output.

## 4. Recent Advances and Trends in Research Paper Recommender Systems: A Comprehensive Survey [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Iratxe Pinedo, Mikel Larrañaga, Ana Arruarte
**Date:** August 12, 2025 (updated September 7, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08828
**Summary:** This survey reviews research paper recommender systems published between November 2021 and December 2024, cataloging the techniques, datasets, and evaluation practices used to help researchers navigate the exponentially growing scientific literature. Unlike earlier surveys that mainly catalog methods, it traces how these techniques are implemented at each stage of the recommendation pipeline, from candidate retrieval through ranking to evaluation. The authors intend it as a reference for researchers building or assessing literature-discovery tools as AI-assisted research infrastructure continues to grow.
