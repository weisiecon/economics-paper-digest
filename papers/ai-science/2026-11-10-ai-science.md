# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-11-10

## 1. Research Collaborations Within and Across Disciplines in NIH-Funded Atlas Programs [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Michael P. Ginda, Yongxin Kong, Katy Börner
**Date:** November 6, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09741
**Summary:** Drawing on 884 NIH award records and 1,255 publications across three human-atlas consortia (HTAN, HuBMAP, SenNet), the authors build co-authorship networks to compare collaboration within single projects, within a consortium component, and across components. They find that cross-component collaborations are rare but disproportionately involve principal investigators and multiple funded projects, and these publications earn significantly higher citation rates in their first five years than single-project work. The results offer funders a template for structuring and evaluating large, multi-disciplinary "Big Science" programs that pool hundreds of researchers.

## 2. Replication Certification at Publication: Checkable by Anyone, Anywhere, in Seconds, Forever [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Gary King, Yiqing Xu, Leo Yang Yang
**Date:** November 6, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09214
**Summary:** The authors propose a "replication certificate": a cryptographically signed record of an author's deposited data, code, and output log, verified once by rerunning the analysis under a random seed derived from the deposit's hash and a blockchain block. This lets anyone, including readers decades later, instantly confirm that a paper's results genuinely came from its deposited materials, without journals needing to redo the analysis or authors needing to spend years fielding replication disputes. Because the archive signs and timestamps the record without rerunning or exposing confidential data itself, the scheme aims to replace today's slow, contentious replication checks with a cheap, tamper-evident standard.
