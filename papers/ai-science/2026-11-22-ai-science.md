## 1. Uncontrolled source titles: When Nature, Science, and PNAS meet Scopus [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Weishu Liu, Ruifeng Zhang, Haifeng Wang
**Date:** November 19, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25627
**Summary:** The authors compare five journal-level retrieval strategies in Scopus against the Web of Science Core Collection for three flagship outlets—Nature, Science, and PNAS—and find substantial, inconsistent fragmentation of how Scopus records each journal's source title, a problem that does not appear to the same degree in Web of Science. They trace likely causes to inherited title variants from upstream data providers, OCR and parsing artifacts, and insufficient cross-validation at the database level. Because bibliometric analyses and research evaluation routinely rely on clean journal-level retrieval, this kind of uncontrolled metadata can quietly bias citation counts and rankings, and the authors recommend stronger source-title normalization and governance by database providers.
