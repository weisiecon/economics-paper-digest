# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-11-01

## 1. Letting AI Formulate the Research Question: Autonomous Research Agents [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Ko Fujimura
**Date:** October 29, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39620
**Summary:** The author builds an autonomous research-agent pipeline that lets an off-the-shelf coding agent pick its own research question, rather than relying on a human-chosen topic, giving it separate skills for formulating and critiquing candidate questions and for peer-reviewing its own completed work. Across 13 development runs and five pre-registered trials, the system reliably moved from question formulation through data collection to a revised, reproducible paper on a laptop with no human input on the research itself, and external AI reviewers who saw only the finished papers judged them near workshop quality. The results and released code offer an early benchmark for how much of the research pipeline, including deciding what to study, autonomous agents can now run end to end.

## 2. Ignore the Writing: A Proposal for Peer Review in the Age of AI [Peer Review & Publication]

**Authors:** Vassilis Kostakos
**Date:** October 27, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38062
**Summary:** The author argues that cheap, fluent AI-written manuscripts have broken peer review's traditional reliance on polished prose as a proxy for underlying research quality, pushing journals toward a future where AI increasingly reviews AI-written papers. To make that loop sustainable, the paper proposes replacing full manuscripts with a short 1500-word brief plus an unlimited, machine-verifiable appendix, so human reviewers judge whether the pursuit is worthwhile, AI models check the experimental record, and humans retain curatorial control over the field's direction. The proposal is a design argument rather than an empirical test, but it reframes what peer review should verify once writing itself is no longer costly or informative.

## 3. Organizational Structure and the Research of U.S. Library and Information Science Schools [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Jiangen He, Wen Lou
**Date:** October 26, 2026 (v2; first posted January 28, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20806
**Summary:** Analyzing 18,048 publications by 1,602 faculty across 44 accredited U.S. Library and Information Science schools from 2013-2026, the authors test whether a school's organizational placement — independent versus housed within computing, education, communication, or arts and sciences — shapes what its faculty study. They find a school's parent unit explains only a small share of the variation in research themes and venues (2-5%), with most differences arising among faculty within the same school, though schools housed with computing skew toward security, networks, and biomedical informatics while those housed with education concentrate on libraries and teaching. Across the period studied, faculty broadly moved away from traditional library research without correspondingly moving toward their parent unit's disciplinary venues, suggesting organizational affiliation has only a loose grip on the direction of a field's research.

## 4. Learning by Collaborating in the Age of AI: Research Mentorship for Polymathic, Workflow-Centered Science [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Tian Zheng
**Date:** October 25, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34606
**Summary:** The author argues that as AI tools lower the cost of technical execution in data-driven research, mentorship needs to shift from teaching technical skills toward cultivating judgment, ethics, and the design of AI-augmented research workflows. The paper proposes "Everyone's Backyard," a two-way mentoring model in which disciplinary experts, AI-native trainees, and community partners teach each other through shared research practice, illustrated with a Columbia University program pairing 20 faculty fellows with 15 student interns across 14 departments. Evaluation data from the program show gains in faculty confidence and intern competence, offering one institutional template for training researchers to work alongside AI rather than simply use it.
