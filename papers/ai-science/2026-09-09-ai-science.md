# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-09

## 1. The Generative AI Gold Rush in Theoretical and Computational Research [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Xiaoshn Nee, Haobo Zhong, Xiaomin Ni
**Date:** September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04872
**Summary:** Using monthly arXiv submission counts across twenty archives from 2018–2026 and a synthetic-control design, the authors detect an anomalous surge in mathematics submissions in 2026 that exceeds a counterfactual built from other fields, alongside a thickening tail of highly prolific authors. They link the timing and pattern of this surge to generative AI diffusion crossing a capability threshold for verifiable research tasks, rather than ordinary field growth. The findings point to verification, selection, and attention as the binding constraints for research governance as AI-assisted output scales up.

## 2. A Visionary Look at Vibe Researching [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Yebo Feng, Yang Liu
**Date:** April 1, 2026 (updated September 3, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00945
**Summary:** The authors coin "vibe researching" for an emerging workflow in which human scientists set direction and judgment while LLM-based agents execute literature review, experiments, analysis, and drafting, drawing an explicit parallel to "vibe coding." They lay out the technical architecture behind this paradigm — multi-agent systems, memory, tool use, and retrieval — and catalog seven current limitations alongside its likely societal upsides and downsides. The paper is offered as a shared map for the research community to reason about responsible adoption rather than as a technical contribution in itself.

## 3. Westlake Scholar: AI-Enhanced Scholarly Discovery over an Institutional Repository [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Junshu Pan, Luodan Zhang, Yifeng Lu, Mengfan Zhao, Ming Luo, Zijie Yang, Yue Zhang, Rui Shang
**Date:** September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05072
**Summary:** The authors describe Westlake Scholar, an open-source platform that layers four AI services — contextual paper reading, direction-guided discovery, expert matching, and AI-generated research timelines — onto a university's institutional repository. The system draws on a shared knowledge layer linking publication records, paper content, and scholar relationships, and has been running live at Westlake University since April 2026. The authors frame it as a governable, institution-controlled alternative to third-party AI discovery tools that preserves provenance and human review.

## 4. Building a research-software catalog with a coding agent: from hackathon prototype to public deployment [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Kazuyoshi Yoshimi, Satoshi Terasaki, Gotai Yamada
**Date:** September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04711
**Summary:** The authors report on turning a hackathon prototype — a catalog of research software built largely by a coding agent — into a public deployment, and on adapting those lessons to a larger, human-curated materials-science software portal. AI coding agents sped up initial development, but the authors found the harder engineering problem was catching silent failures — plausible-looking but incomplete or wrong outputs — rather than outright crashes. They conclude that AI-assisted research infrastructure still needs explicit validation, monitoring, and curated documentation to be trustworthy.

## 5. FOXDEN: FAIR Services for AI-Ready Scientific Datasets [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Valentin Kuznetsov, Werner M. Sun, Keara Soloway, Rolf Verberg, Katherine S. Shanks, Suchismita Sarker, David S. Butcher, Kelly E. Nygren
**Date:** September 2, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03105
**Summary:** The authors present FOXDEN, a set of cyberinfrastructure tools built at a synchrotron facility for attaching rich, machine-readable metadata and provenance to raw and processed scientific datasets so they can be published with DOIs and used in AI workflows. The system is deployed at two experimental facilities and is intended as a foundation for future "agentic" scientific workflows that consume this data automatically. The paper illustrates the infrastructure work — metadata standards and provenance tracking — needed before AI can be reliably applied to experimental science.
