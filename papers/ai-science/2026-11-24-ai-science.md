## 1. The Landscape of Generative AI in Information Systems: A Synthesis of Secondary Reviews and Research Agendas [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Aleksander Jarzębowicz, Adam Przybyłek, Jacinto Estima, Yen Ying Ng, Jakub Swacha, Beata Zielosko, Lech Madeyski, Noel Carroll, Kai-Kristian Kemell, Bartosz Marcinkowski, Alberto Rodrigues da Silva, Viktoria Stray, Netta Iivari, Anh Nguyen-Duc, Jorge Melegati, Boris Delibašić, Emilio Insfran
**Date:** Updated November 19, 2026 (originally posted March 12, 2026)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11842
**Summary:** The authors synthesize 28 secondary studies and research-agenda papers on generative AI in Information Systems research published since 2023, using bibliometric mapping and thematic analysis. They find GenAI offers real productivity and innovation gains but that adoption is constrained by technical unreliability, ethical risks, and a governance vacuum, exposing a persistent gap between fast-moving AI capabilities and slower-adapting social and regulatory structures. They propose redirecting IS scholarship from simply measuring GenAI's impacts toward actively shaping how AI capabilities, organizational routines, and governance co-evolve.

## 2. A Framework for AI-Ready Data in Earth and Environmental Science [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Tobias Weigel, Jan Bumberger, Danu Caus, Christian Chwala, Romy Fösig, Sonja Hänzelmann, Sibylle K. Hassler, Beate Hetenyi, Ralf Kunkel, Katharina Loewe, Ulrich Loup, Yueling Ma, Stefan Neuhaus, Kaleb Phipps, Tino Polen, Maren Rebke, Björn L. Saß, David Schäfer, Timm Schoening, Emanuel Söding, Paula Stock
**Date:** November 20, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27313
**Summary:** A multidisciplinary group of data providers, infrastructure developers, and AI practitioners develops a 25-criterion framework for assessing how "AI-ready" Earth and environmental science data are, extending the FAIR principles to cover AI-specific needs such as provenance, bias documentation, and uncertainty characterization. The framework emerged from a community-driven process combining a literature review with a survey of scientists using machine-learning methods, and was tested against a range of representative environmental use cases. The authors argue AI-readiness should be treated as a property of transparency and fitness for purpose rather than a fixed quality bar, aiming to give the research-data community a shared basis for supporting AI-enabled scientific workflows.
