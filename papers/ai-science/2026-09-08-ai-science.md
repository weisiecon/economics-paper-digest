# AI & Economics of Science Papers — 2026-09-08

## 1. The Generative AI Gold Rush in Theoretical and Computational Research [Econ of Science]

**Authors:** Xiaoshn Nee, Haobo Zhong, Xiaomin Ni
**Date:** September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04872
**Summary:** Using 2,080 monthly arXiv-archive observations from 2018 through 2026 and a synthetic-control design fit through the end of 2025, the authors show mathematics submissions jumped sharply in 2026 — new listings running roughly 34% above 2025 levels and 10-15% above a synthetic counterfactual — with the surge spread across nearly all math subfields and concentrated among a thickening tail of highly prolific authors. Placebo tests across other arXiv archives and alternative pre-periods rule out ordinary trend growth, pointing instead to generative AI crossing a capability threshold that let researchers rapidly multiply verifiable outputs. The authors argue the episode exposes verification, selection, and attention as the binding constraints on research governance once generative AI can inflate production this fast.

## 2. A Visionary Look at Vibe Researching [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Yebo Feng, Yang Liu
**Date:** April 1, 2026 (v1); revised September 3, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00945
**Summary:** The authors name and define "vibe researching," a division of labor in which a human sets direction and exercises critical judgment while LLM-based agents execute the literature review, experiments, analysis, and manuscript drafting — a research-side analog of "vibe coding" in software engineering. They lay out the multi-agent architectures (memory, tool use, retrieval-augmented generation) that make this possible, catalog seven technical limitations, and weigh the practice's likely benefits against risks such as eroded methodological rigor and accountability gaps. The paper is meant as a shared reference point so the research community can debate responsible adoption from common ground rather than talking past each other.

## 3. Westlake Scholar: AI-Enhanced Scholarly Discovery over an Institutional Repository [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Junshu Pan, Luodan Zhang, Yifeng Lu, Mengfan Zhao, Ming Luo, Zijie Yang, Yue Zhang, Rui Shang
**Date:** September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05072
**Summary:** The authors describe Westlake Scholar, a deployed system that layers four AI services — contextual paper reading, direction-guided discovery, expert matching, and AI-generated scholar chronologies — onto a university's institutional repository, drawing on a shared knowledge layer that links publications, content, and scholar relationships. Live at Westlake University since April 2026, the platform is designed to keep provenance, human review, and institutional governance intact even as AI mediates how researchers find related work and people. The case offers a concrete route for universities to add AI-driven discovery to existing repository infrastructure without ceding control over how it is governed.

## 4. Building a research-software catalog with a coding agent [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Kazuyoshi Yoshimi, Satoshi Terasaki, Gotai Yamada
**Date:** September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04711
**Summary:** The authors turned a three-day hackathon prototype — a catalog of research software built largely by a coding agent — into a publicly deployed tool, then documented the extra engineering (adversarial review, data-quality checks, validation, publication safeguards) needed to make it reliable. They found the coding agent's most consequential failures were not crashes but silent ones that produced plausible-looking yet incomplete or wrong metadata, and they carried these lessons into a larger retrieval-agent prototype for the MateriApps portal that combines curated metadata, documentation, and vector search. The case argues that AI-assisted research infrastructure still needs curated data and ongoing human review, even when coding agents make initial development fast.

## 5. FOXDEN: FAIR Services for AI-Ready Scientific Datasets [AI for Science]

**Authors:** Valentin Kuznetsov, Werner M. Sun, Keara Soloway, Rolf Verberg, Katherine S. Shanks, Suchismita Sarker, David S. Butcher, Kelly E. Nygren
**Date:** September 2, 2026 (v1); revised September 4, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03105
**Summary:** The authors present FOXDEN, cyberinfrastructure built at the Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source for attaching structured metadata and provenance records to raw, reduced, and analyzed scientific datasets and publishing them with DOIs so they meet FAIR standards. They describe its architecture and its live deployment at CHESS and the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory, positioning it as a foundation for agentic scientific workflows that need machine-readable, well-provenanced data to operate on. The paper is a systems contribution rather than an empirical study, illustrating how facilities are retrofitting data pipelines to make AI-ready science practical.
