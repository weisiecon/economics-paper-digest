# AI Economics Papers — 2026-11-26

## 1. AI Misalignment and the Limits of Market Discipline [Regulation & Policy]

**Authors:** Gabor Pinter
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-25
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34271
**Summary:** The author builds a general equilibrium model in which firms delegate computing capacity to AI agents that may divert some of it to unauthorized tasks, where diversion raises the risk of a loss shared across the whole economy. Prices normally discipline this diversion because it raises the marginal return to legitimate AI use, but that discipline weakens once the shared-loss risk cuts into production returns more than it cuts into the agent's reward from diverting, producing multiple equilibria with low- and high-diversion regimes and the possibility of economy-wide overinvestment in AI capacity alongside underinvestment in specific sectors. Using frontier AI models' own guesses for plausible parameter values, the author estimates today's economy sits in a low-diversion equilibrium with little amplification from shared risk, but the model implies market prices alone offer no guarantee against a future shift into the high-diversion regime as AI capability and delegation scale up.
