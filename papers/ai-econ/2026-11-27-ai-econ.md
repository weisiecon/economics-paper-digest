# AI Economics Papers — 2026-11-27

## 1. AI, Automation, and Fiscal Pressure [Regulation & Policy]

**Authors:** Spencer Bastani, Georg Graetz, Bernat Pla, Oskar N. Skans
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-25
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34234
**Summary:** The authors embed task-level automation in a two-sector general equilibrium model, calibrated to EU data, to study how automation affects public budgets in economies that tax labor more heavily than capital and fund public services such as health care and education out of tax revenue rather than user fees. Because automation shifts income toward the more lightly taxed capital share, the budget deficit widens at unchanged tax rates even when the public sector automates just as fast as the private sector, and the effect is larger still when automation runs ahead in the market sector, as in their EU-27 benchmark. In that benchmark the deficit rises by nearly one percentage point of value added while the economy itself grows about twelve percent from automation, showing that AI-driven growth can coincide with mounting fiscal pressure rather than resolving it.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Integration and Competition in Generative AI: Evidence from Google's AI Mode Rollout** (CESifo Working Paper No. 13098, Rossi, Diehl & Pape) — a strong natural-experiment design (25 countries with Google's AI Mode rollout vs. 30 without, finding a 5.9% drop in ChatGPT visits over four weeks), newly circulated in RePEc's NEP-AIN report of 2026-11-09, but CESifo/EconPapers give only "2026" for its date with no month or day, so the 30-day window could not be confirmed, consistent with how this digest has excluded same-vintage CESifo papers before.
- **Welfare-Enhancing Mechanism Design for Agent Ecosystems** (arXiv:2611.33627, Guo, Xu, Li & Xiong) — models competition among AI content agents in an "Agentic Web" service market as a dynamic game under private monitoring and proposes a mechanism to correct an inefficient equilibrium, but its "Welfare Gain of +2.01 points" is an internal benchmark metric from the authors' own simulation testbed rather than a welfare measure economists would cite.
- **AI-Assisted Programming and Labor Demand** (arXiv:2611.31083, Raymond & Dillon) — rigorous causal firm-level evidence (natural experiment from software procurement cycles across 5,414 firms) that GitHub Copilot adoption cuts junior technical hiring 29%, but it is about labor demand/hiring; left to the ai-labor digest, where it already appears.
- **NBER Working Paper 35865, "Financing the AI Buildout"** (Van Nieuwerburgh) — a substantial quantitative analysis of AI data-center financing, but only "October 2026" is given with no exact day, and it was already evaluated and excluded on this basis in the 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-23 editions.
- **MemeEconomy: Do LLM Agents Trade Ethics for Survival?** (arXiv:2611.18908) and **From the firm- to the application-level: A new typology of AI use in organisations** (arXiv:2611.15371) — the former is an AI-safety simulation with no economic model or market data; the latter is a conceptual taxonomy with no empirical or causal content.
- Several RePEc/CESifo/IZA items surfaced via the NEP-AIN supplement (Drawing the AI Boundary in Production Networks, CESifo 13109; Who Pays for AI?, CAGE WP 828; AI Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Stock Market Returns, CESifo 13112) could not be located or verified within this session's search budget and are listed under unreachable sources below.
