# AI Economics Papers — 2026-10-20

## 1. Competition and Diversity in Generative AI [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Manish Raghavan
**Date:** first posted 2024-12-11; revised 2026-10-17
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08610
**Summary:** The author builds a game-theoretic model of producers who can adopt generative AI tools while competing with one another for customers or attention, and tests its predictions empirically by having language models play Scattergories, a game that rewards answers that are both correct and unique. Competition turns out to counteract the homogenization that generative AI is widely observed to cause: when producers compete, they are pushed toward novel or unique content, so competitive markets select for diverse AI models and content rather than monoculture. A striking implication is that a generative AI model which scores well on an isolated benchmark can still fail to create value once deployed in a competitive market, meaning benchmark performance alone is a poor guide to how an AI model will actually be used and valued.

## 2. Human-AI Productivity Paradoxes: Modeling the Interplay of Skill, Effort, and AI Assistance [Productivity & Growth]

**Authors:** Ali Aouad, Thodoris Lykouris, Huiying Zhong
**Date:** first posted 2026-05-12; revised 2026-10-17
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11350
**Summary:** The authors model workers of varying skill who choose how much effort to exert on a task when assisted by generative AI, building in both the endogenous development of worker skill over time and the possibility that AI assistance is unreliable. They show this combination can generate a genuine productivity paradox: more AI assistance can lower, rather than raise, measured output, and they derive interpretable conditions that pin down exactly when this happens. Tracking the model forward, they also find that AI assistance can drive long-run skill polarization across the workforce, offering a theoretical account for why empirical evidence on AI's productivity effects has been so mixed across different settings.
