# AI Economics Papers — 2026-11-06

## 1. Strategic Integration of Artificial Intelligence in the C-Suite: The Role of the Chief AI Officer [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Marc Schmitt
**Date:** first posted 2024-04-30; revised 2026-10-29
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10247
**Summary:** Drawing on what the author calls the first archival census of executive AI leadership, this paper tracks how companies have assigned accountability for AI strategy since 2023 and builds a role-design theory around three properties that set AI apart from earlier enterprise technologies: diffuse judgment, upstream governance needs, and constant change. It finds that dedicated Chief AI Officer roles have diffused sharply, that firms are about as likely to create a new CAIO position as to fold AI duties into an existing executive mandate, and that CAIO adoption clusters where strong digital-leadership structures already exist. Contrary to commentary describing the CAIO as a fad role, the author finds executive tenures in the position are not unusually short, suggesting the governance structures firms are adopting for AI are proving durable rather than transitional.

## 2. Narratives to Numbers: Large Language Models and Economic Policy Uncertainty [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Ethan Hartley
**Date:** first posted 2025-11-22; revised 2026-10-26
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17866
**Summary:** Rather than treating AI-constructed economic data as free of its own methodological choices, this paper asks how building the widely used Economic Policy Uncertainty index with large language models instead of keyword search changes what the index measures. An LLM fine-tuned on human audit labels catches nearly twice as many EPU-relevant news articles as keyword matching and holds up better against noisy or drifting text, but varying the classifier, decision threshold, and aggregation method yields indices that disagree not just on how much uncertainty exists but on what kind of uncertainty they capture. Because the estimated relationship between policy uncertainty and stock returns shifts with these modeling choices, the paper argues that adopting machine-generated data in economic research transfers consequential judgment calls from economists to upstream model design, which should be made explicit rather than treated as a black box.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Unmitigated? How are the world's largest companies responding to AI risks?** (arXiv:2611.04278) — a large, carefully constructed disclosure audit (200+ companies, 272-expert Delphi study), but descriptive rather than causal, structural, or welfare-analytic; consistent with this digest's 2026-11-04 edition, left out on relevance grounds.
- **Could It Have Fit Anything? Restrictiveness of Economic Models, LLMs, and AI Workflows** (arXiv:2602.07688, Fudenberg, Gao & You) — a rigorous methodological contribution comparing how restrictive LLM/AI procedures are versus structural economic models at predicting choice behavior, but its subject is model-comparison methodology rather than AI's effect on markets, firms, diffusion, regulation, or welfare.
- **Learning AI Agents, Market Makers, and Stress in Automated Markets** (arXiv:2611.01799) — a theoretically careful model of AI trading agents and automated market makers, but it is a financial-market-microstructure paper rather than one about AI as an economic sector or technology.
- **Practice Makes Perfect: Tacit Knowledge, Generative AI, and Returns to Schooling and Experience** (arXiv:2610.36008) — strong causal evidence (CPS panel, exposure index, placebo tests) that generative AI is repricing the returns to schooling and experience, but it is primarily a labor-economics/wage-structure paper; left to the ai-labor digest, which did not pick it up this run either.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (arXiv:2610.28830) — a conceptual/theoretical framework with no empirical test or causal design; already excluded on the same grounds in today's ai-labor digest.
- **Measuring Geopolitical Alignment and Economic Growth** (arXiv:2507.04833) — a strong local-projections growth paper, but AI/LLMs are used only as a tool to construct the geopolitical-alignment measure; the substantive question (alignment and growth) is not about AI's economic effects.
- **The Fallacy of Sustainable Generative AI: Limitations in EU Environmental Regulation of Data Centres** (arXiv:2607.22604) — a qualitative policy critique of EU data-centre efficiency metrics, with no causal identification or formal economic model.
- **AI and the research assistant: Evidence from economics working papers** (arXiv:2608.08437) — cross-topic with the ai-science digest (AI's role in research production), left there.
