# AI Economics Papers — 2026-11-10

## 1. AI as Shared Business Infrastructure: Firm Growth and the Distribution of Gains [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Lu Fang, Jinhan Liu, Huilan Xu, Zhe Yuan
**Date:** November 2026 (NBER Working Paper No. 36003)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w36003
**Summary:** Using complete transaction and adoption records from a Chinese e-commerce platform that embedded a shared generative-AI system into every firm's business software in 2024, the authors use a matched difference-in-differences design (confirmed with instrumental variables) to estimate how adoption affects small sellers' sales. They find adoption raises monthly sales by about 52%, driven mainly by more orders, with the largest proportional gains going to the smallest firms, and trace the effect to AI lowering the cost of routine listing work and reducing information frictions between buyers and sellers. At the market level, categories with worse pre-existing information frictions see more entry and more new products, indicating AI expands markets rather than just reallocating sales among incumbents.

## 2. Generative AI, Managerial Expectations, and Economic Activity [Productivity & Growth]

**Authors:** Manish Jha, Jialin Qian, Michael Weber, Baozhong Yang
**Date:** first posted 2024-10-04; revised 2026-11-09
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03897
**Summary:** The authors use generative AI to extract managers' forward-looking economic expectations from more than 120,000 corporate earnings-call transcripts, constructing firm-, industry-, and economy-wide "AI Economy Score" indices. These scores forecast GDP growth, production, employment, and wages five or more quarters ahead, outperforming survey-based forecasts and existing expectation measures, with predictive power concentrated among historically more accurate, less-biased managers. The results suggest executives' AI-extracted expectations carry genuine forward-looking information about aggregate economic activity rather than noise, with implications for both macroeconomic forecasting and firm-level decision-making.

## 3. Strategic Competition and Convergence in LLM Leaderboards [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Naveen Raman, Fei Fang, Yiling Chen
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-06
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09780
**Summary:** This paper models competition among LLM developers on public leaderboards as a game in which firms allocate resources across capability dimensions to maximize win rate, then asks when such competition produces "monoculture" (converging capability profiles) versus persistent differentiation. The model shows that similar production functions push developers toward similar capability profiles, while comparative advantage can sustain distinct ones, and diminishing returns make the competitive frontier increasingly crowded over time. Using real data from LM Arena and OpenRouter, the authors confirm that developers invest more where they lag rivals while still protecting category-specific strengths, implying that intensifying competition among AI firms need not erase product differentiation.

## 4. The Rise of AI Search: Implications for Information Markets and Human Judgement at Scale [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Sinan Aral, Haiwen Li, Rui Zuo
**Date:** first posted 2026-02-13; revised 2026-11-07
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13415
**Summary:** Running 24,000 search queries across 243 countries in 2024 and 2025, the authors document the rapid global rollout of AI-generated search answers (such as Google's AI Overviews), which expanded from 7 to 229 countries over the period and now answer the large majority of certain query types that were almost untouched by AI a year earlier. They show AI search systematically surfaces fewer long-tail sources and more low-credibility, ideologically skewed content than traditional search, with substantial cross-country variation in exposure driven by opaque corporate and governmental policy choices. The authors argue these patterns affect the economic incentives to produce new information and raise market concentration in information production, warranting closer regulatory and economic scrutiny of AI search as it reshapes the information ecosystem.
