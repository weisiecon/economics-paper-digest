# AI Economics Papers — 2026-10-17

## 1. AI Information Synthesis and Content Polarization [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Chang Liu, Yuting Zhu
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-13
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17674
**Summary:** The authors build a game-theoretic model in which content producers split limited informational capacity between broadly valuable, common content and audience-specific, differentiated content, while consumers can instead turn to an AI tool that synthesizes dispersed information. As AI's reliability rises from low to high, its effect moves through three regimes: it does nothing at low reliability, merely disciplines producer prices at intermediate reliability, and once consumers start substituting toward it, pushes producers to reallocate capacity toward differentiated content, raising polarization. This happens because AI draws away the consumers who were only loosely matched to any single producer, leaving each producer with a narrower, more partisan remaining audience to cater to — an effect that strengthens with AI reliability and weakens as competition among producers increases, showing that a technology which itself delivers more balanced information can still make the overall content market more polarized.

## 2. Breaking News Out of the Filter Bubble: Generative AI Search Diversifies Collective Attention and Raises Shared Information Consumption [Trade & Welfare]

**Authors:** Heeseung Andrew Lee, Dokyun Lee, Gwanhoo Lee, Dongwon Lee
**Date:** first posted 2026-09-30
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38946
**Summary:** In a randomized field experiment with 37,561 readers at the Washington Post, the authors tested whether adding AI-generated answers with citations above conventional search results changes what people read, addressing fears that generative AI search would narrow readers to the same few topics or isolate them in ever-smaller filter bubbles. They find the opposite on both counts: AI answers increase the share of topics readers have in common even as they shift consumption away from the most popular topics toward less-read ones, both within individual readers and across the audience as a whole. The AI answers themselves account for most of the added common ground, since readers absorb them without needing to click through, while more frequent searching offsets lower per-search article reading to leave total information consumption roughly unchanged or slightly higher — evidence that generative AI search can diversify and still partially unify what a news audience collectively knows.

## 3. Safety Research and the Allocation of Frontier Compute [Regulation & Policy]

**Authors:** Li Gan
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-09
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14305
**Summary:** The author builds a frontier-takeoff model to ask who should bear the cost of compute spent on AI safety research — customers who lose some service capacity, or capability researchers who accept a slower path to a finished frontier model. The optimal split is governed by a threshold in "demand capacity," the share of compute customers can eventually use once the frontier is reached: research-funded safety is worthwhile only when demand capacity falls below that threshold, which itself rises as the service budget allowed to customers shrinks, while customer-funded safety should be front-loaded early in the takeoff. In a calibrated example with a tight service budget, shifting safety spending onto customers early in the takeoff cuts the modeled probability of catastrophe from 75 to 51 percent, nearly matching the best rule found even when that rule was also allowed to slow capability research — giving policymakers a quantitative way to think about who should pay for AI safety and when.

## 4. Does AI Help or Harm? Endogenous Information Acquisition with AI Advice [Trade & Welfare]

**Authors:** Kexin Chen, Tao Lin, Jianwei Huang
**Date:** first posted 2026-09-28
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35137
**Summary:** The authors study a decision-maker who receives AI advice before choosing how much costly information to gather and then acting, asking when such advice raises versus lowers the quality of the resulting decision. They show the answer hinges on a convexity property of the value generated by the decision-maker's optimal information-gathering response: when that value function is convex, any AI signal weakly helps regardless of the prior, but once it is not convex, even a simple binary AI signal can make the decision-maker worse off by causing them to stop valuable information acquisition altogether ("concentrated shutdown") or to redirect limited attention away from the distinctions that matter most ("attention dilution"). The framework delivers sharp conditions — AI advice is always safe in two-state problems, but a minimal three- or four-state example is enough to produce harm — giving a precise account of why more informative AI advice does not always translate into better human decisions.

## 5. Non-Linear Pricing Restores Tractability for a Data Seller [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Bhaskar Ray Chaudhury, Jugal Garg, Eklavya Sharma, Jiaxin Song
**Date:** first posted 2026-09-29
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36589
**Summary:** The authors study a data seller who prices access to multiple datasets to maximize revenue from buyers who have budgets and must trade off accuracy gains against cost. Prior work showed that when each dataset must carry a single linear price, finding the revenue-optimal prices is computationally intractable; this paper shows that allowing more general, non-linear pricing functions not only increases revenue but — surprisingly — also restores tractability, since the optimal pricing functions turn out to be simple piecewise-linear and convex schedules computable in polynomial time. Because the total number of "kinks" in these optimal schedules is bounded by the number of buyers rather than the number of datasets, most prices end up effectively linear whenever datasets outnumber buyers, and simulations on real data quantify how much revenue a data seller leaves on the table by restricting itself to linear pricing.
