# AI Economics Papers — 2026-10-27

## 1. The Delegation Divide: An Open Index of AI-Agent Delegability across Occupations and Countries [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Cristian Espinal Maya, Santiago Jiménez Londoño
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-22
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30752
**Summary:** The authors build the Agent Delegability Index by having two language models rate all 18,796 O*NET task statements against a preregistered rubric of delegation levels and barriers, then map tasks to ISCO-08 occupations and weight them by ILO employment data across 146 countries. They find that the share of task time delegable to AI agents rises sharply with income — from 22.5% in low-income countries to 48.7% in high-income ones — while full delegation of any task remains rare and concentrated on short (around 12-minute) tasks, and that this delegability measure tracks observed AI usage closely rather than revealing a wider gap for middle-income countries as the authors had expected. The result is a validated, cross-country measure showing that today's AI-agent capabilities would mechanically widen rather than narrow global gaps in who can offload work to AI, a distributional finding with direct bearing on how the gains from agentic AI diffusion are likely to be distributed internationally.
