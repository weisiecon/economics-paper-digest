# AI Economics Papers — 2026-10-15

## 1. Creative destruction: Human-AI competition in an experimental market [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** John Conlon, Peter Schwardmann
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-12
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18921
**Summary:** The authors run an experiment in which participants design computer games with an AI coding agent and other participants buy them, testing whether a durable human advantage in taste or a consumer preference for human-made goods can preserve a role for human producers. Across three studies (N = 1,025) they find neither: AI-only designs beat human producers, including professional creatives, and disclosing that a game is human-made does not raise willingness to pay. The result is direct experimental evidence that, in this market, AI capability growth displaces rather than complements human producers, with no offsetting demand-side premium for human involvement.

## 2. Beyond Independent Values: Optimal Auctions with Correlated Valuations for LLM-Native Advertising [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Shiman Sheng, Mingyu Guo
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-10
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14646
**Summary:** The authors design AffMyerson, an auction mechanism for sponsored placements inside conversational-AI answers that explicitly models advertisers' correlated willingness to pay, since rival bidders reading the same conversation tend to value the slot similarly. They prove the mechanism stays truthful and individually rational under this correlation structure and derive closed-form pricing that runs fast enough for real-time use. Against independent-value benchmarks their mechanism captures 4.5-9.2% more revenue depending on the setting, showing that ignoring bid correlation leaves money on the table in the emerging market for LLM-native advertising.

## 3. AI as Coordination-Compressing Capital: Task Reallocation, Organizational Redesign, and the Regime Fork [Productivity & Growth]

**Authors:** Alex Farach
**Date:** first posted 2026-02-17; revised 2026-10-01
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16078
**Summary:** The author models AI as capital that compresses the coordination costs managers incur per subordinate, relaxing span-of-control limits for a fixed pool of managers. The model shows that whether firms end up with positive or zero coordination-cost floors determines long-run managerial wage dispersion, and that inequality can follow a non-monotone path—rising above its eventual level before settling—when managers with different floors absorb AI capital at different rates. The paper gives a structural account of how AI-driven organizational redesign could reshape within-firm wage inequality independent of workers' underlying skill distribution.

## 4. PriceBench: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Price, Quality, and Brand Preferences in LLM Booking Agents [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Pavel Kireyev
**Date:** first posted 2026-09-25
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31468
**Summary:** The author uses a discrete-choice (logit) model to recover the implicit price, quality, and brand preferences revealed when 28 LLMs from 8 providers choose among real hotel listings across 3,600 booking tasks. More capable models hold stronger and more internally consistent preferences, while weaker models default to position effects exploitable by whoever controls listing order; price sensitivity varies by more than an order of magnitude across providers, shifting mean booked price from $247 to $393 on identical tasks. As LLMs increasingly act as purchasing agents, the findings show provider-specific agent preferences will materially affect market prices and outcomes, and that these preferences must be measured rather than assumed.
