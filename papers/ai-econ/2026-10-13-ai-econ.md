# AI Economics Papers — 2026-10-13

## 1. When Less Is More: Managing AI Adoption with Adaptive Incentive Design [Adoption & Diffusion]

**Authors:** Jie Gong, Jiayi Hou, Jin Li, Fei Pu, Xinjue Yao
**Date:** first posted 2026-09-26
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32859
**Summary:** The authors study a large medical-device company that mandated roughly 5,000 employees to submit at least 200 monthly queries to an internal AI assistant, then exploit a staggered rollback of the quota to 100 queries across branches to identify its effects. First-time use rose sharply after the mandate but usage bunched at the threshold and nearly a third of queries were repeated or off-task, and lowering the target cut query volume by 30%, almost entirely by eliminating gaming rather than genuine use, while sales employees' monthly sales rose 7%. The findings identify incentive-scheme recalibration as a distinct and economically consequential margin of AI technology adoption, separate from simply mandating use.

## 2. Delegate Pricing to Algorithms: When Slow and Steady Wins the Race [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Galit Ashkenazi-Golan, Edward Plumb, Clemens Possnig, Yufei Zhang
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-08
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11675
**Summary:** The authors model firms that delegate pricing to learning algorithms by choosing each algorithm's learning rate in a continuous-time pricing game, evaluating firms' payoffs as the discounted profit accrued along the entire learning path rather than only at equilibrium. They prove a dichotomy: under strategic substitutability firms prefer the fastest algorithms to exploit a slowly adjusting rival, but under strategic complementarity an excessively fast algorithm provokes a faster competitive response and destroys transitional profits, so firms optimally choose sluggish algorithms even though the competitive price is dominant in the underlying stage game. The result shows how the strategic design of pricing algorithms, not just their equilibrium behavior, can itself sustain supra-competitive profits during a prolonged adjustment path.

## 3. Mechanism Design for AI Overviews: Creator Incentives and Long-Term Profit [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Yihang Wu, Jiajun Tang, Jinfei Liu, Haifeng Xu, Fan Yao
**Date:** first posted 2026-01-30; revised 2026-09-27
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22493
**Summary:** The authors build a game-theoretic model of content creators who exert costly effort under a search engine's AI Overviews, which answer queries directly and divert traffic from creators, and design two interventions: a citation mechanism that credits sources within the overview and a compensation mechanism paying creators directly. Calibrating the model to real click data, they show AI Overviews erode long-term search-engine profit by discouraging creator effort and driving user attrition, but that profit-maximizing versions of either mechanism can restore and even increase long-term profit relative to an unmitigated AI Overview across a range of realistic scenarios. The paper gives platforms and regulators a mechanism-design basis for addressing the two-sided market harm AI-generated answers impose on content ecosystems.

## 4. Understanding Interfirm AI Talent Flow Networks through Online Professional Profiles [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Donghang Li, Yunhan Zheng, Alok Prakash, Shenhao Wang, Jinhua Zhao
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-06
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08264
**Summary:** Using roughly 535 million employment records across 58 countries, the authors reconstruct the inter-firm network through which AI talent circulates between 2010 and 2022 and relate firms' network position to their market value. They find AI talent inflows are increasingly concentrated among a small set of leading firms with stronger preferential attachment than general labor mobility, yet central network positions change hands far more often than in non-AI networks, and event-study estimates show that sharp improvements in a firm's network position are followed by higher enterprise value relative to matched firms on similar pre-event trajectories. The results indicate that firms' AI advantage depends not just on the stock of talent they hold but on their position in its ongoing circulation across the market.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Evolution of Market Microstructure in the Age of AI** (arXiv:2609.33058) — a survey tracing market-design impossibility results through AI-agent trading; no new empirical or theoretical result of its own.
- **Marrying Pricing and Advertising with LLMs** (arXiv:2610.09985) — an actor-critic reinforcement-learning algorithm for joint price/ad generation, validated only on synthetic and simulated demand; an algorithm-design contribution rather than an economic finding.
- **PriceBench: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Price, Quality, and Brand Preferences in LLM Booking Agents** (arXiv:2609.31468) — a diagnostic benchmark of LLM booking-agent preferences across 28 models; documents price variation an AI purchasing agent induces but offers no causal or structural market analysis.
- **Is AI Widening the Wage Gap? A Hybrid Agentic Simulation for Labor Equity** (arXiv:2609.33367, revised 2026-10-06) — primarily a labor-market wage-inequality question (and already flagged in the AI-labor digest); a synthetic agent-based simulation rather than observed market outcomes.
- **When AI Companions Disappear: Relational Continuity and Collective Contestation during China's National AI Regulatory Transition** (arXiv:2609.15482) — a qualitative social-media content analysis of user reactions to a regulatory transition; no economic outcome or market-level design.
- **Validity, Reliability, and Transparency in Artificial Intelligence Regulation** (arXiv:2608.05800, revised 2026-10-05) — a legal/normative framework for evidentiary standards in AI regulation; no economic model or outcome data.
- **Auditing AI-Washing in German Startups: A Mixed-Methods Study of Marketing Claims and Perceptions** (arXiv:2610.11788) — a mixed-methods audit of startup marketing claims; descriptive, no market or firm-outcome analysis.
- **Artificial Intelligence as an Economic, Environmental, Geopolitical, and Social Transformation** (arXiv:2609.21632) — a discursive overview essay with no original research design.
- **AI Adoption Across Mission-Driven Organizations** (arXiv:2510.03868, revised 2026-09-27) — a qualitative interview study (15 experts) of adoption barriers; no causal or market-level design.
