## 1. Understanding AI Provider Recommendations in Local Service Markets [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Hazem Ibrahim, Yasir Zaki
**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18341
**Summary:** Ibrahim and Zaki audit AI assistants' provider referrals across four registry-backed local service markets (clinicians, care facilities, financial advisers, and restaurants) in the 100 largest U.S. metro areas, checking every recommendation against the relevant official registry under three retrieval conditions: an open-weight model, a proprietary model with no web search, and the same proprietary model with search. Without search, both models mostly fabricate providers that do not match any registry entry, recommended financial advisers carry misconduct disclosures at 3.6 times the base rate, and real matches skew toward the largest metros; turning on search lifts match rates to 64-71%, pushes the misconduct rate below the registry base rate, and largely erases the metro-size skew, though restaurant picks still show a strong review-count premium over a near-flat rating premium. The results show that whether an AI-mediated referral market delivers trustworthy, place- and quality-neutral recommendations hinges on the retrieval architecture an AI firm chooses rather than on the underlying model, with no signal to users when an answer was produced without verification — a market-design and consumer-protection question for regulators of AI intermediation in local service markets.
