## 1. Learning AI Agents, Market Makers, and Stress in Automated Markets [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Irene Aldridge
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-01
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01799
**Summary:** This paper models traders in automated markets as AI agents defined by their reward functions, and analyzes the feedback their learning behavior creates on a constant-product automated market maker. It shows that herd-dominated and arbitrage-dominated markets of equal size can look identical on standard risk metrics (volatility, value-at-risk, volume) while their stress episodes differ enormously in duration, and derives the data requirements for distinguishing the two regimes from trade statistics alone. The analysis implies that spread-earning market makers stabilize prices but make liquidity fragile with discontinuous collapse, and that maker-taker pricing — not a uniform fee — is the right policy instrument for managing this trade-off as AI-driven trading agents proliferate.
