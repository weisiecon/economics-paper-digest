# AI Economics Papers — 2026-11-12

## 1. Competition in Generative AI: Evidence from Consumer Demand [Firms & Competition]

**Authors:** Mark L. Egan, Dominic Russel, Claire Shi, Vincent Yao
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-26
**Source:** NBER Working Paper 35953, https://www.nber.org/papers/w35953
**Summary:** Using transaction-level card data covering 174 million U.S. consumers and 114 billion transactions, the authors document that only about 2.9% of consumers pay for a major generative AI service as of April 2026, that most paying users choose low-cost plans, and that multi-homing, low annual retention, and switching around major model releases all point to weak provider differentiation. A structural model of demand and supply confirms this reading: consumers are highly price-sensitive, value AI as a category more than any specific provider, and increasingly treat competing services as substitutes. The result implies that unless quality differentiation widens, competitive pressure in the generative AI market will keep providers' pricing power limited and let consumers capture most of the surplus — a market-structure finding relevant to ongoing debates about whether frontier AI will tip toward concentration or stay competitive.

Only 1 candidate cleared the relevance bar today; this is a thin-day result, not a broken run.
