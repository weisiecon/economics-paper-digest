# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-09-22

## 1. Monocultural Biases: Correlated biases in large language models lead to unequal systemic exclusion rates in hiring [Hiring]

**Authors:** Matthew Bone, Fabian Stephany, Maria del Rio-Chanona
**Date:** Submitted 2026-08-26
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22169
**Summary:** Bone, Stephany, and del Rio-Chanona audit ten LLMs' simulated hiring callback decisions, comparing base and post-trained versions of each model to see whether the industry's convergence on a small set of foundation models is homogenizing who gets screened out of hiring. They find post-trained models agree with each other far more than base models do, and this added cross-model consensus roughly triples the share of applicants rejected by every model in the set (from 5.6% to 17.3%), with age discrimination against older applicants driving most of the increase. Because employers using different vendors' LLM hiring tools increasingly reach the same verdicts, the result points to a market-level risk of correlated exclusion that a jobseeker cannot escape simply by applying through a different AI-screened employer.
