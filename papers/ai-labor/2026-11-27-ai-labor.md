# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-27

## 1. AI and the Division of Labor in Public Sector Jobs and Teams [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Andrew Ledingham, Michael Hollins, Matthew Lyon, David Gillespie, Umar Yunis-Guerra, Jamie Siviter, David Duncan, Oliver P. Hauser
**Date:** first posted 2025-12-05; revised 2026-11-25
**Source:** arXiv:2512.05659
**Summary:** Using 283,484 UK Civil Service vacancies, the authors extract 2.7 million individual tasks and estimate each one's automation potential with an ensemble of eight foundation models, then simulate role- and team-level redesign based on that exposure. They find that task-level differences within roles account for 65.3% of total exposure variance (versus just 4.0% from profession and grade combined), and that team-level redesign merges overlapping roles — cutting distinct roles by 17.7% while headcount falls by only 6.4%, with the eliminated posts concentrated in junior and middle grades so that surviving teams skew toward senior staff. The paper gives a granular, task-based account of how AI exposure is likely to reshape public-sector job structure and seniority composition well before it shows up in aggregate headcount numbers.
