# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-09-17

Only one paper cleared the relevance bar today; see below for why the rest of the pool fell short.

## 1. Linguistic Triggers of Gender and Racial Bias in Open-Weight LLMs Applied to Recruitment [Hiring]

**Authors:** Kosuke Kitahara, Nobuhiro Yamaguchi
**Date:** Submitted 2026-09-16
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18106
**Summary:** Kitahara and Yamaguchi run the first systematic multi-model audit of six open-weight LLMs (Llama 3.2, Mistral, Gemma 3, Qwen 3, Phi 3, DeepSeek-R1) simulating both recruiter screening and job-seeker application decisions, varying job-posting language and candidate-persona labels across controlled experiments. They find agentic-toned postings depress recruiter scores for female candidates while communal language partly offsets the penalty, and that coded-exclusion language sharply lowers recruiter scores for non-White candidates while also deterring non-White applicants from applying in the first place; a label-ablation test isolates the explicit demographic persona label as the causal driver, corroborated by word-embedding association tests. The paper turns these results into a concrete pre-deployment bias-audit protocol tied to EU AI Act Annex III and US EEOC adverse-impact rules, underscoring the discrimination exposure created as open-weight LLMs spread through hiring pipelines.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Agentic Inequality** (arXiv:2510.16853) — a conceptual framework on disparities in power and opportunity from unequal access to autonomous AI agents; no empirical labor-market design or finding.
- **AI and the Economy: An Economic Examination of Production, Distribution, Firms, Labor, and Welfare** (arXiv:2609.01263) — a broad review essay on AI's economic channels, including labor markets; no original empirical analysis.
- **Prepared Or Unprepared? Evaluating Healthcare Workforce Readiness for Clinical Adoption of Artificial Intelligence in Nigeria** (arXiv:2609.19096) — a cross-sectional attitudes/knowledge survey of 761 clinicians; descriptive associations only, no causal design or employment/wage outcome.
- **IUU+DB: Tracking Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated Fishing, Seafood Fraud, and Labor Abuse through LLM-driven Information Extraction** (arXiv:2606.18181) — an LLM-based incident-database tool that surfaces labor-abuse cases; a system/dataset paper with no quantified worker-outcome finding.
- **Digital Engagement, Income Disparities, and Job Seeking in the United States since 2010** (arXiv:2511.05294) — a well-identified NLSY97 panel study linking internet-use frequency to income and employment attachment, but the exposure is plain internet access, not an AI or automation channel.
