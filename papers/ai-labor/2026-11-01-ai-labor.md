# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-01

No qualifying papers today: the arXiv API harvest (econ.GN, econ.EM, cs.CY, sorted by last-updated date) returned 2,000 entries (API cap reached), of which 1,001 fell within the 30-day window; 67 matched the labor-market keyword filter, and 17 were dropped as duplicates already covered in this folder, leaving 50 non-duplicate candidates. Of those, the great majority had no genuine labor-market content at all (cultural-heritage, cybersecurity, robocall-detection, and education-tooling papers picked up on the "automat-"/"task" keywords), and the handful of genuine AI-and-labor or labor-economics candidates each failed on one dimension or another — see "Excluded, borderline." NBER's RSS feeds were unreachable again (see below), so the supplement tier ran via web search; it surfaced only AI-labor working papers (NBER, IZA, CEPR) dated well outside the 30-day window, and none closer to today's date.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Game theory of academic effort and hiring in the age of AI** (arXiv:2610.35802) — a genuine game-theoretic model of how AI affects signaling and hiring equilibria in academic labor markets, but the same paper was already selected in `papers/ai-science/2026-10-28-ai-science.md`; its labor-market framing is not distinct enough from that coverage to warrant a second entry.
- **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance: Disentangling Informational and Affective Pathways** (arXiv:2610.25548) — a well-designed randomized field intervention at a consulting firm linking AI-mediated listening to worker performance, but it is a research proposal with no data collected or results reported yet.
- **Occupational Uniqueness and Wage Growth** (arXiv:2610.28133) — a rigorous task-based panel analysis (O*NET/DOT, 1977–2018) linking occupational task structure to wage growth, but the paper has no AI or automation mechanism; task change is studied generically.
- **The GenAI Who Knew Too Little: Revisiting Transactive Memory Systems in Human GenAI Collaboration** (arXiv:2610.28343) — a qualitative interview study (14 knowledge workers) of human-GenAI collaboration dynamics; no quantified worker or labor-market outcome.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (arXiv:2610.28830), **Asymmetric Information in Wage Contracts** (arXiv:2610.06827), **Adopt-an-AI-Researcher** (arXiv:2602.09846), **Workplace Ambiguity and Technology Support for Autistic and IDD Workers** (arXiv:2610.21236) — repeat candidates already excluded in the 2026-10-31 digest on the same grounds (no empirical data, no AI mechanism, or qualitative-only design); no new evidence changes that assessment.
- **AI and Labor Market Outcomes: Evidence from China** (Fang, Lin & Liu, IZA DP18740), **Job Transformation, Specialization, and the Labor Market Effects of AI** (Freund & Mann, IZA DP18565), **Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce** (Baslandze et al., CEPR DP21313), **Does Automation Lower the Labor Share?** (Barany, Patel & Siegel, CEPR DP21700) — found via web search, all with genuine AI-labor content, but posted well outside the 30-day window (April–July 2026 or earlier).
