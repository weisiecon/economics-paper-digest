# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-22

No qualifying papers today: the arXiv API harvest (econ.GN, econ.EM, cs.CY; sorted by last-updated date) returned 2,000 entries, 1,115 in the 30-day window, and 70 after keyword filtering. Of those, 40 appeared to be non-duplicates against the literal dedupe command, but a closer bare-ID cross-check (since prior digests mix `arxiv.org/abs/ID` and `arXiv:ID` source formats) showed 36 were already covered or excluded in earlier digests — including two strong-looking candidates ("Advancing AI Capabilities and Evolving Labor Outcomes," arXiv:2507.08244, and "AI Exposure and the Changing Responsibilities in Job Postings," arXiv:2611.09870, both already published in the 2026-11-11 digest). The remaining 18 genuine non-duplicates had no AI-and-labor-market content at all (education/learning-analytics studies, financial-market microstructure, cybersecurity, wildlife-trade and cultural-heritage ML applications picked up on the "automat-" keyword).

## Excluded, borderline

- **Task Expansion with Generative AI: Experimental Evidence on Middle-Skilled, Early-Career Workers** (Brosch, Langer, Lergetporer, Mueller, Pfeifer & Tschoepe; IZA DP18999 / CESifo Working Paper No. 13045) — a strong, directly on-topic field experiment (673 German IT apprentices randomly given generative-AI access; productivity rises 26-31 percentage points on tasks beyond formal training), but both the IZA and CESifo listings give only "October 2026" with no exact day, so eligibility for this window (2026-10-23 to 2026-11-22) cannot be confirmed, consistent with how a similarly undated IZA paper was excluded on 2026-11-21.
- **Productivity and employment effects of AI adoption in Europe** (Aldasoro et al.; CEPR DP21082) — on-topic (12,000+ European firms, IV design, +4% labor productivity with no adverse firm-level employment effect), but dated January 2026, well outside the window.

## Sourcing note

Both NBER RSS feeds were unreachable as feeds: `papers.rss` redirected (301) to `www2.nber.org/papers.rss`, which 404'd, and `programs/ls/papers.rss` returned HTTP 200 but served an HTML program-listing page (meta-refresh redirect), not feed XML — consistent with recent digests. Within the 4-search/4-fetch supplement budget, IZA, NBER, and CEPR web searches surfaced only the two candidates above with confirmable (if ultimately disqualifying) dates; all other hits (NBER w33509, w33867, w34386, w31846, w33777; IZA DP18267, DP18767, DP18740; CEPR VoxEU columns on Denmark and French automation) were either well outside the 30-day window or lacked day-level dates and were not pursued further given the budget.
