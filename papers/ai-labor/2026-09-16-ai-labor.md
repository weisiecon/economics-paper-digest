# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-09-16

## 1. Do job seekers value procedure in AI hiring only for error correction? Evidence from a conjoint experiment [Hiring]

**Authors:** Chuyao Wang, Patrick Sturgis, Daniel de Kadt
**Date:** Submitted 2026-09-14
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16390
**Summary:** Wang, Sturgis, and de Kadt run a preregistered paired-profile conjoint experiment with 1,919 US job seekers, each making eight choices between AI hiring-screening systems that independently vary decision authority, error rate, explanation, opt-out, appeal, and independent bias audit. They find that the value applicants place on appeal rights, opt-out, and bias audits does not rise as wrongful-rejection rates worsen, and that simply having a human involved moves choices about as much as cutting wrongful rejections from 30% to 10%. The results show applicants value procedural safeguards partly for their own sake, not just as insurance against errors, so improving an AI screening system's accuracy does not substitute for procedural rights job seekers can invoke.

## 2. Complements or Substitutes? Technology Adoption and the Demand for Clinical Care: Evidence from Automated Insulin Delivery [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Moslem Rashidi, Cristina Ugolini, Gianluca Fiorentini
**Date:** Submitted 2026-09-11
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12976
**Summary:** Using staggered adoption of automated insulin delivery (AID) among 1,608 type-1-diabetes patients in the Italian National Health Service, the authors combine risk-set coarsened exact matching with a staggered event-study design to test whether patient-facing automation reduces or increases demand for professional clinical labor. They find AID adoption raises routine outpatient engagement substantially (a 16.3-percentage-point rise in diabetologist visits after one semester, 39.3 points after four), rather than displacing it. The pattern fits a task-based view in which automation substitutes for routine dosing decisions while complementing clinical labor on interpretive, adjustment, and supervisory tasks, reorganizing rather than simply reducing skilled work.
