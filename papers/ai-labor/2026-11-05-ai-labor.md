# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-05

## 1. Pay and Beyond: The Evolving Rewards for AI Skills [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Alejandra Castaneda, Matthew Bone, Fabian Stephany
**Date:** first posted 2025-07-27; revised 2026-11-04
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20410
**Summary:** Using roughly ten million U.S. online job vacancies posted between 2018 and 2025, the authors track how demand for AI skills, the wage premia attached to them, and the non-monetary benefits advertised alongside them (tuition assistance, parental leave, remote work, inclusive-culture language) evolved before and after generative AI's late-2022 debut. They find AI-skill demand and wage premia rose sharply across small, mid-sized, and S&P 500 firms alike, with wage premia growing fastest at smaller firms, and AI postings are markedly more likely than comparable non-AI roles to advertise non-pay benefits, especially at SMEs and large firms. The wage-benefits relationship also shifted after generative AI spread: the prior gap between S&P 500 firms (where high pay and benefits were complementary) and SMEs (where they substituted) narrowed, suggesting the competition for AI talent is reshaping total-compensation strategy well beyond salary alone.

## 2. Dull, Dirty, Dangerous? Working Conditions and the Limits of Occupational AI Exposure [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Siyi Yu
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-04
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06888
**Summary:** Using 21 O*NET working-condition items across 754 U.S. occupations, the author tests whether jobs more exposed to AI, by both an LLM-based measure and the established AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index, are actually the dull, dirty, or dangerous jobs that automation is often promised to relieve. Higher AI exposure is associated with somewhat better working-condition scores under both measures, but once physical demands, required education and training, and wages are controlled for, the association weakens; the adjusted confidence interval includes zero for the LLM-based measure and stays below zero for AIOE. The paper cautions that exposure scores alone cannot establish whether AI will actually deliver "relief" for workers in unpleasant jobs, since that depends on workers' own preferences and on which specific interventions follow from exposure.

## 3. Today's Work, Tomorrow's Workforce: Incentivizing Learning in AI-Assisted Work [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Moritz Diener, Daniel Hendriks, Philipp Spitzer, Gerhard Satzger, Joshua Holstein, Wei Sun
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-04
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06346
**Summary:** The authors extend a standard "accept-or-solve" model of AI-assisted work to a multi-period setting in which workers' own skills evolve depending on whether they rely on AI or practice independently on each task. They show that workers who only maximize immediate payoffs can become trapped never acquiring skill as novices, or losing it as experts, even while AI-assisted output looks accurate throughout, whereas workers who value their future skill will sometimes forgo a more accurate AI answer to keep practicing. They then characterize the incentive payments organizations need to sustain independent work and skill retention, finding that such work can become self-sustaining past a skill threshold and that targeting a higher skill level can, counterintuitively, lower the total incentive cost.

## 4. Improving Entrepreneurial Skills by Practicing with AI [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Amir Globerson, Anisha Choudhury, Anna Iurchenko, Avinatan Hassidim, Cathy Cheung, Elizabeth Kim, Femi Olanubi, Gabby Espinosa, Gaby Andrews, Gal Elidan, Hannah Lieber, Hairong Mu, Katherine Chou, Nir Kerem, Niv Efron, Rajvi Kapadia, Rena Levitt, Rotem Yulzary, Shashank Agarwal, Tracey Lee-Joe, Victor Martinez, Yael Haramaty, Yishay Mor, Yossi Matias
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-01
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02016
**Summary:** The authors adapt Vantage, a Google Research platform, into a practice environment for entrepreneurship that pairs learners with simulated AI peers engineered to play out realistic business scenarios and press learners to exercise critical thinking, creativity, and negotiation. In a randomized controlled trial embedded in summer entrepreneurship programs run with The Network for Teaching Entrepreneurship, students who practiced with Vantage scored significantly higher on their final Capstone Business Plans than control students who only had access to standard AI chat tools. The result offers early causal evidence that structured AI-peer practice, rather than mere AI access, is what drives measurable gains in durable, hard-to-teach entrepreneurial skills.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Experimentally Validated Digital Twins: Evaluating Workforce Training Programs in the Age of Big Data** (arXiv:2611.03938) — a rigorous machine-learning program-evaluation method validated against 37 RCTs, but its contribution is a causal-inference technique for job-training evaluation rather than a finding about AI's effect on labor-market outcomes.
- **Labor Market Returns to Education and Intra-family Educational Inequality: Theory and Experimental Evidence** (arXiv:2611.02959) — a theory and lab-in-the-field experiment on sibling educational-inequality; access to an AI course is only the incentivized allocation good, with no AI/automation mechanism driving the labor-market finding.
- **Harder Than the Task: From Technical to Organizational Difficulty in AI Benchmarking** (arXiv:2611.03494) — a conceptual taxonomy for organizational task difficulty in AI benchmark design, not an empirical finding about worker or labor-market outcomes.
- **Game theory of academic effort and hiring in the age of AI** (arXiv:2610.35802) — a game-theoretic model of academic hiring signaling; narrow to research-sector hiring rather than general labor markets, and already surfaced in the ai-science digest.
- **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance: Disentangling Informational and Affective Pathways** (arXiv:2610.25548) — directly on-topic in design, but remains a research proposal for a planned field experiment with no results yet; already excluded for the same reason in the 2026-11-03 digest.
- **Workplace Ambiguity and Technology Support for Autistic and IDD Workers** (arXiv:2610.21236) — a qualitative interview study (11 caregivers) with an AI co-design exercise; no quantified labor-market outcome or causal research design.
