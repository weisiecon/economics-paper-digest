# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-10-27

No papers cleared the relevance bar today. The in-window, keyword-matched arXiv harvest consisted entirely of theoretical/conceptual frameworks with no empirical labor-market data, papers with no AI or automation mechanism, or qualitative/proposal-stage studies without causal or quantified findings, and the NBER/IZA supplement turned up only candidates whose exact dates could not be confirmed as falling inside the window.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Game Theory of Academic Effort and Hiring in the Age of AI** (arXiv:2610.35802) — a game-theoretic model of academic hiring with equilibrium classification and learning simulations; no empirical labor-market data.
- **Occupational Uniqueness and Wage Growth** (arXiv:2610.28133) — a rigorous O*NET/DOT panel study (1977-2018) linking task-structure change to wage growth; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (arXiv:2610.28830) — a conceptual AI-Job Dynamics Framework from sociotechnical systems theory; no empirical data or causal design.
- **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance: Disentangling Informational and Affective Pathways** (arXiv:2610.25548) — a well-designed randomized field intervention at a consulting firm, but still a research proposal with no results reported.
- **AI as Coordination-Compressing Capital: Task Reallocation, Organizational Redesign, and the Regime Fork** (arXiv:2602.16078, revised 2026-10-01) — a theoretical model of AI-driven managerial-wage dispersion with "numerical illustrations, not estimates"; no empirical findings.
- **Does the Gender Wage Gap Originate at Labor Market Entry? Evidence from South Korea** (arXiv:2608.03153, revised 2026-10-12) — a careful selection-corrected quasi-experimental design; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **Workplace Ambiguity and Technology Support for Autistic and IDD Workers** (arXiv:2610.21236) — an 11-caregiver interview study with an AI co-design activity; qualitative, no causal design or quantified labor-market outcome.
- **Digital Labor Platforms as Infrastructure of Extractivism** (arXiv:2609.37614) — a critical/qualitative chapter on AI data-annotation labor drawing on mixed-methods fieldwork; no causal identification or quantified outcome.
- **The Early Impacts of AI on Employment among Recent College Graduates** (Fairlie & Wu; NBER WP 35796 / IZA DP 18945) — directly on-topic causal evidence (no relative rise in new-graduate unemployment, with a positive interaction between remote-work availability and AI exposure), but both NBER and IZA list only "September 2026" with no exact day, so eligibility for this 30-day window remains unconfirmed.
- **The Wage Effects of Generative AI** (Azar, Giné & Sanz-Espín) — strong quasi-experimental evidence of wage declines (largest for junior workers) from GenAI occupational exposure using OEWS and Revelio data, but the paper is dated August 21, 2026, outside the 30-day window, with no confirmed September/October revision.
