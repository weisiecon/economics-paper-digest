# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-11

## 1. Advancing AI Capabilities and Evolving Labor Outcomes [Employment]

**Authors:** Jacob Dominski, Tyler Boes, Yong Suk Lee
**Date:** first posted 2025-07-11; revised 2026-11-09
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08244
**Summary:** The authors build an occupational AI exposure score tracking capability jumps from generative chatbots to reasoning models and agents, and link it to Current Population Survey data from January 2023 through June 2026 using first-difference and fixed-effects designs (with 2017-2022 as a placebo period). Occupations with larger exposure increases show no change in overall employment or unemployment, but their workforce composition shifts: workers under 30 without a college degree decline by about 8 percent per standard deviation of exposure change (driven by reduced entry, not higher exit), while college graduates aged 30-49 gain full-time employment and the remaining workforce becomes older and more educated. The findings suggest AI's earliest labor-market effects show up in who gets hired rather than in aggregate job counts.

## 2. AI Exposure and the Changing Responsibilities in Job Postings [Hiring]

**Authors:** Dora Zhao, Lin Ai, Pranav Khadpe, Siddharth Suri, Scott Counts
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-06
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09870
**Summary:** Using activity-level AI exposure measures derived from Copilot and Claude usage, the authors map the responsibilities listed in 50,000 job postings from 33 firms (2023-2025) onto O*NET's standardized work-activity taxonomy to track how employer demand for specific tasks has shifted. Explicit mentions of AI in postings rose 66%, mostly outside the actual listed responsibilities, while highly AI-exposed work activities declined only mildly as a share of advertised responsibilities even as they increasingly came paired with explicitly AI-related duties. The pattern suggests employers are not yet dropping AI-exposed tasks from job descriptions so much as redefining them to require AI fluency.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Choosing Complexity: Cognitive Ability, Overconfidence, and Task Choice** (arXiv 2611.13259) — rigorous lab experiment on task-complexity choice, but no AI linkage.
- **Wage-Setting Constraints and Firm Responses to Demand Shocks** (arXiv 2512.19622) — strong firm-level wage-setting evidence (Portugal, Norway), but no AI linkage.
- **Good Looks and the Academic Job Market: Measuring Attractiveness with Vision Embeddings** (arXiv 2611.10447) — real hiring-outcome data, but AI (vision embeddings) is used only as a measurement instrument for attractiveness, not as the subject of study.
- **Double Machine Learning for Inequality Decompositions** (arXiv 2511.13433) — methodological advance on Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition; no new empirical labor-market finding.
- **A Taxonomy-Driven Synthetic Dataset for Retrieval Effectiveness and Fairness Evaluation in Hiring** (arXiv 2611.09178) — a synthetic benchmark/dataset paper for HR retrieval systems; no worker or labor-market outcome.
- **From Dialogue to Task: Cognitive Engagement With Generative AI** (arXiv 2611.11435) — rich study of student-AI dialogue and task outcomes, but about university learning tasks, not the labor market.
- **The GenAI Who Knew Too Little** (arXiv 2610.28343) — qualitative interview study (14 knowledge workers); not an economics-style quantitative design.
