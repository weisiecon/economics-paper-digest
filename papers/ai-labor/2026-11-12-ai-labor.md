# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-12

No papers qualified for inclusion today: the arXiv harvest and supplemental searches turned up candidates that were either already logged in a prior digest, lacked a genuine AI linkage, or were conceptual/proposal-stage work without an empirical labor-market finding.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Choosing Complexity: Cognitive Ability, Overconfidence, and Task Choice** (arXiv 2611.13259) — rigorous lab experiment on task-complexity choice, but no AI linkage.
- **Wage-Setting Constraints and Firm Responses to Demand Shocks** (arXiv 2512.19622) — strong firm-level wage-setting evidence, but no AI linkage.
- **From Dialogue to Task: Cognitive Engagement With Generative AI** (arXiv 2611.11435) — rich study of human-AI dialogue, but about university learning tasks, not the labor market.
- **Double Machine Learning for Inequality Decompositions** (arXiv 2511.13433) — methodological advance on Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition; no new empirical labor-market finding.
- **Good Looks and the Academic Job Market: Measuring Attractiveness with Vision Embeddings** (arXiv 2611.10447) — real hiring-outcome data, but AI (vision embeddings) is used only as a measurement instrument, not as the subject of study.
- **A Taxonomy-Driven Synthetic Dataset for Retrieval Effectiveness and Fairness Evaluation in Hiring** (arXiv 2611.09178) — a synthetic benchmark/dataset paper for HR retrieval systems; no worker or labor-market outcome.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (arXiv 2610.28830) — directly on topic (AI, job autonomy, skill requirements), but purely theoretical/conceptual with no empirical analysis.
- **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance** (arXiv 2610.25548) — well-designed randomized field intervention, but the paper is an explicit research proposal with no results yet.
- **LLM Recommenders Produce Demographic Inequality Through Content Preferences** (arXiv 2610.41854) — rigorous controlled experiment on AI-driven inequality, but concerns content-recommendation visibility, not employment/wages/hiring.
- **Harder Than the Task: From Technical to Organizational Difficulty in AI Benchmarking** (arXiv 2611.03494) — relevant taxonomy of organizational task difficulty for AI agents, but conceptual, with no empirical worker-outcome finding.
- **Workplace Ambiguity and Technology Support for Autistic and IDD Workers** (arXiv 2610.21236) — AI co-design touches the workplace, but a small qualitative interview study (11 caregivers), not an economics-style design.
- **When Algorithms Time Workers: Speed-Quality Tradeoffs in a Randomized Field Experiment** (Kim & Mitrofanov, SSRN 7554058, via secondary digest) — a large-scale randomized field experiment on algorithmic management, which would otherwise qualify strongly, but SSRN cannot be fetched directly (403) and no RePEc/IDEAS listing could be found to confirm a posting date within the window; excluded rather than guessed.
- **Investment Stimulus, Automation, and Skill Demand** (Park & Suh, SSRN 7602245, via secondary digest) — automation/skill-demand finding of likely interest, same SSRN-access and unconfirmed-date problem as above; excluded rather than guessed.
- **CEPR DP21082 (Aldasoro et al.), DP21337, IZA DP18565/18767/18945, NBER w34984/w35796** — relevant AI-labor papers surfaced via web search, all independently confirmed (NBER site or prior digests) to be dated January-September 2026, outside the 30-day window.

## Sourcing note

NBER's RSS feeds (papers.rss redirects to a 404; programs/ls/papers.rss likewise unreachable) were unreachable, consistent with recent runs. The arXiv harvest API succeeded on the first attempt (2000 entries returned, 1118 within the 30-day window, 69 passing the keyword filter, 42 surviving dedupe), but none cleared the relevance bar for a worker- or labor-market-outcome finding with a recognizable economic research design. The supplemental web search budget (4 searches, 6 fetches) surfaced two promising SSRN field-experiment papers whose posting dates could not be confirmed without direct SSRN access, so they were excluded rather than guessed.
