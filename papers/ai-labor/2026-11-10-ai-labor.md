# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-10

Only 2 papers cleared the relevance bar today; written as found rather than padded to 5.

## 1. AI Exposure and the Changing Responsibilities in Job Postings [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Dora Zhao, Lin Ai, Pranav Khadpe, Siddharth Suri, Scott Counts
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-06
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09870
**Summary:** Analyzing roughly 50,000 online job postings from 33 firms between 2023 and 2025, the authors map job responsibilities onto O*NET's Intermediate Work Activities and link them to activity-level AI exposure measures derived from actual Copilot and Claude usage patterns. They find explicit mentions of AI in job ads rose 66%, but mostly outside the actual listed duties, while work activities with greater AI exposure became only mildly less prevalent in postings even as those same activities were increasingly bundled with explicitly AI-related responsibilities. The pattern suggests employers are reshaping who they hire to perform AI-exposed tasks, favoring candidates who can do that work alongside AI, rather than simply eliminating those tasks from job ads.

## 2. AI as Shared Business Infrastructure: Firm Growth and the Distribution of Gains [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Panle Jia Barwick, Lu Fang, Jinhan Liu, Huilan Xu, Zhe Yuan
**Date:** first posted 2026-11 (NBER WP No. 36003)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w36003
**Summary:** Using complete transaction and AI-usage records from a major Chinese e-commerce platform that embedded a generative AI system into small merchants' operating software in 2024, the authors apply a matched difference-in-differences design, confirmed with instrumental-variable estimates, to firms with annual sales under RMB 5 million. Adopting firms saw monthly sales rise 51.6%, driven mainly by a 47% jump in orders and concentrated among the smallest sellers, as AI let them reach more customers, update listings faster, and produce richer product content while spending less staff time per order. The authors attribute the gains to AI lowering the cost of routine work and reducing information frictions, and show the effect expands overall market demand rather than reallocating sales from non-adopters to adopters, evidence that shared AI infrastructure can lift small-firm labor productivity broadly.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Good Looks and the Academic Job Market: Measuring Attractiveness with Vision Embeddings** (arXiv 2611.10447) — AI (vision embeddings) is used only as a measurement tool; the economic finding is about appearance affecting academic hiring via recommendation letters, not AI's effect on labor markets.
- **From Dialogue to Task: How Cognitive Engagement With Generative AI Relates to Post-Dialogue Activity and Task Outcomes** (arXiv 2611.11435) — about university students' engagement with AI tutoring companions in coursework, not worker or labor-market outcomes.
- **Labor Market Returns to Education and Intra-family Educational Inequality** (arXiv 2611.02959) — a strong lab-in-field experiment on sibling educational investment, but AI-course access is only an incidental allocation token in the design, not the phenomenon studied.
- **LLM Recommenders Produce Demographic Inequality Through Content Preferences** (arXiv 2610.41854) — a rigorous audit of demographic bias, but about social-media content ranking rather than employment or labor-market outcomes.
- **Double Machine Learning for Inequality Decompositions** (arXiv 2511.13433, revised 2026-11-07) — a general econometric method for wage-gap decomposition; no AI-labor finding.
- **Does the Gender Wage Gap Originate at Labor Market Entry? Evidence from South Korea** (arXiv 2608.03153) — careful causal wage-gap study, but machine learning is used only as a selection-correction tool, not an AI/automation topic.
- **Workplace Ambiguity and Technology Support for Autistic and IDD Workers** (arXiv 2610.21236) — a qualitative study (11 caregiver interviews) with an AI co-design activity, not an economics-style research design.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (arXiv 2610.28830) and **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance** (arXiv 2610.25548) — conceptual framework and unexecuted research proposal respectively; already excluded in prior digests for lack of empirical results.
- CEPR DP21313, DP21337 and IZA DP19056 — already covered in the 2026-11-06/2026-11-09 digests.
