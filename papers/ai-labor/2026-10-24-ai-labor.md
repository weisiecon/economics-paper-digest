# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-10-24

Only one paper cleared the relevance bar today; the rest of the in-window, keyword-matched harvest either lacked any AI/automation mechanism, lacked a recognizable economist's research design, or had already been covered and excluded in earlier digests, so this digest is shorter than usual rather than padded.

## 1. How Heavily Are PhD Students Offloading Their Scientific Writing to AI? Evidence from US STEM PhD Dissertations [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Daniel P. Gross, Dror Shvadron, Hansen Zhang
**Date:** first posted 2026-09-30; revised October 2026 (exact day not stated by source)
**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35859
**Summary:** The authors measure AI-generated writing in a near-census of US STEM PhD dissertations filed 2019-2026 and link graduates to their subsequent employment, testing whether offloading scientific writing to large language models erodes the expertise that writing is meant to build. AI-generated text is essentially absent before 2023, appears in 29% of 2026 dissertations and is growing quickly, with heavier use among students from non-English-speaking countries and at lower-ranked programs; within the same program and cohort, graduates whose dissertations show AI writing are less likely to enter academic research and more likely to take non-tenure-track or industry jobs, a pattern strongest among US-origin students. The paper gives labor economists a concrete, large-sample early-career outcome tied to AI task offloading, suggesting AI may raise research productivity even as it displaces part of the writing work through which research expertise is normally formed.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Measuring the prevalence and direction of innovation: a pilot approach using LLMs** (Garg, Lambert, Martin & Medvedev; CEP Discussion Paper No. 2216 / CAGE WP 825) — a strong firm-level design (LLM-coded innovation survey across ~600,000 firms in 40 countries, linking a product-innovation index to a 5% higher measured TFP residual and finding labor-augmenting innovation more common than labor-saving) that would be directly on-topic, but the paper is dated only "September 2026" with no exact day, so eligibility for the 30-day window (cutoff 2026-09-24) could not be confirmed without guessing.
- **The Early Impacts of AI on Employment among Recent College Graduates** (Fairlie & Wu; NBER WP 35796 / IZA DP 18945) — a causal CPS-based design directly on topic, but both NBER and IZA still give only "September 2026" with no day; excluded for date uncertainty, as in six earlier digests.
- **AI adoption, productivity and employment: evidence from European firms** (Aldasoro, Gambacorta, Pál, Revoltella, Weiss, Wolski; CEPR DP21082 / BIS WP 1325 / EIB WP) — firm-level instrumented design finding AI adoption raises productivity ~4% with no adverse employment effect and higher wages at adopting firms, but dated January 2026, well outside the window; excluded again as in the 2026-10-23 digest.
- **Has AI Widened Employment Gaps? Tracking Early-Career Employment by Occupational Exposure in Norway** (Hernæs & Kostøl, IZA DP 18767) — a strong administrative-data tracking design, resurfaced via web search, but already covered in the 2026-07-20 digest and in any case dated June 2026, outside the window.
- **Occupational Uniqueness and Wage Growth** (Vestrelli & Fronzetti Colladon, arXiv:2610.28133) — a large O*NET/DOT panel (1977-2018) linking occupational task-structure change to wage growth; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **Does the Gender Wage Gap Originate at Labor Market Entry? Evidence from South Korea** (Kim, arXiv:2608.03153, revised 2026-10-12) — a careful selection-corrected wage-gap design; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **Skill, Luck, or Imitation? What Actually Pays in Creator Markets** (Tariq, arXiv:2609.32925) — a rich empirical study of YouTube creator earnings with 17 robustness checks; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance: Disentangling Informational and Affective Pathways** (Tamme & Levin, arXiv:2610.25548) — a well-designed proposed randomized field intervention on AI-enabled workplace listening; a research proposal with no results yet.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (Shirangi & Pittinsky, arXiv:2610.28830) — a purely conceptual framework paper with theoretical propositions only; no empirical findings. Previously excluded from the 2026-10-22 digest.
- **AI as Coordination-Compressing Capital: Task Reallocation, Organizational Redesign, and the Regime Fork** (Farach, arXiv:2602.16078, revised 2026-10-01) — a purely theoretical model of AI-driven organizational redesign with numerical illustrations only; no empirical validation. Previously excluded from four earlier digests.
- **Economic Governance of Autonomous Agents and Robots** (Syed et al., arXiv:2609.32476) — a fiscal-policy design proposal (CivicDividendOS) illustrated on a hypothetical logistics setting; no empirical data or market outcome. Previously excluded from two earlier digests.
- **Digital Labor Platforms as Infrastructure of Extractivism** (Posada, arXiv:2609.37614) — a critical-theory book chapter on AI data-annotation labor; qualitative/conceptual, no quantified labor-market design. Previously excluded from two earlier digests.
