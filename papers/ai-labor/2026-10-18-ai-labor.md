# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-10-18

No candidate combined clear AI relevance with a research design an economist would recognize today; this digest contains no entries as a result.

## Excluded, borderline

- **AI as Coordination-Compressing Capital: Task Reallocation, Organizational Redesign, and the Regime Fork** (arXiv:2602.16078, revised 2026-10-01) — a purely theoretical model of AI-driven organizational redesign and managerial wage dispersion with numerical illustrations only; no empirical validation. Previously excluded from the 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-16 digests for the same reason; this revision (v4) is unchanged.
- **Adopt-an-AI-Researcher: Bootstrapping Industry-Academia Collaboration to Identify and Prioritise Generative AI Use Cases** (arXiv:2602.09846, revised 2026-10-05) — a single-firm qualitative case study (six-week engagement) on generative-AI use-case discovery; process-oriented, no worker/market outcome. Previously excluded from the 2026-10-16 digest for the same reason; this revision is unchanged.
- **The Early Impacts of AI on Employment among Recent College Graduates** (IZA DP No. 18945 / NBER, Fairlie & Wu) — a strong CPS difference-in-differences and event-study design on AI and early-career unemployment. Excluded again for date uncertainty: the IZA listing, its EconPapers mirror, and NBER all give only "September 2026" with no day, and no page opened this session states an exact date; a specific day found in earlier secondary coverage is a search-result snippet, which the anti-fabrication rule bars from being treated as authoritative. Previously excluded from the 2026-10-16 digest on the same grounds.
- **Has AI Widened Employment Gaps? Tracking Early-Career Employment by Occupational Exposure in Norway** (IZA DP No. 18767, Hernaes & Kostøl) — a strong administrative-data design (Norway private-sector employment through February 2026, tracked by occupational AI-exposure quintile against a public dashboard), surfaced via web search. Excluded as dated June 2026, well outside the 30-day window (cutoff 2026-09-18).
- **GenAI exposure and wages** (Azar, Giné & Sanz-Espín; occupational wage data (OEWS) and Revelio job-posting data linking a low-to-high GenAI-exposure move to a ~4.9% wage decline concentrated among junior workers, with no detectable employment effect), surfaced via web search through a conference-site PDF mirror. Excluded as dated August 21, 2026, outside the 30-day window; a possible more recent revision could not be confirmed within this session's search/fetch budget.
