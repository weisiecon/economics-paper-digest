# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-10-25

No papers cleared the relevance bar today. The in-window, keyword-matched arXiv harvest and the RePEc/NEP-LMA supplement turned up AI-and-work papers, but each either lacked a real AI/automation mechanism, lacked a causal or firm/market-level research design an economist would recognize, or fell outside the 30-day window once an exact posting date could be confirmed; several others had already been excluded in earlier digests for the same reasons and nothing new changes that call.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Generative AI and Posted Labor Demand: Volume, Seniority and Timing** (Cerina, Nobili & Rosso; CRENoS WP 26/13) — a strong causal design (368 million Lightcast US job postings, 2016-2025, usage-based LLM exposure measure, finding an 8.6% posting-volume decline in AI-exposed occupations after ChatGPT's release with no seniority-composition break) that would otherwise be a lead candidate, but it was announced in the NEP-LMA report of 2026-08-10 and no later posting date could be confirmed, placing it well outside the 30-day window (cutoff 2026-09-25).
- **Generative AI and Task Recomposition in Entry-Level IT Work: A Worker-Side Task-Level Measure** (Zisler & Backes-Gellner; University of Zurich Economics of Education WP 258) — a worker-survey-based task-reallocation measure finding AI complementarity rather than substitution for entry-level IT workers, also announced in the 2026-08-10 NEP-LMA report with no later date found; excluded for falling outside the window.
- **Maintaining Human Verification Capacity under Automation** (Gan & Gan, arXiv:2609.38459) — a framework paper on automation bias and the erosion of independent human-checking skill, illustrated only with a numerical example; no field data, firm outcomes, or causal identification.
- **The GenAI Who Knew Too Little: Revisiting Transactive Memory Systems in Human GenAI Collaboration** (Meske, Hermanns & Brachten, arXiv:2610.28343) — a qualitative interview study (14 knowledge workers) on human-GenAI collaboration dynamics; no labor-market outcome data or causal design.
- **Workplace Ambiguity and Technology Support for Autistic and IDD Workers** (Curry et al., arXiv:2610.21236) — a qualitative accessibility/HCI study with an AI co-design workshop; no employment or wage outcome data.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Job Design: A Dynamic Framework** (Shirangi & Pittinsky, arXiv:2610.28830) — a purely conceptual framework paper with theoretical propositions only; no empirical findings. Previously excluded from multiple earlier digests.
- **Occupational Uniqueness and Wage Growth** (Vestrelli & Fronzetti Colladon, arXiv:2610.28133) — a large O*NET/DOT panel linking occupational task-structure change to wage growth; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **Does the Gender Wage Gap Originate at Labor Market Entry? Evidence from South Korea** (Kim, arXiv:2608.03153) — a careful selection-corrected wage-gap design; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **AI-Mediated Employee Voice and Performance: Disentangling Informational and Affective Pathways** (Tamme & Levin, arXiv:2610.25548) — a proposed randomized field intervention on AI-enabled workplace listening; no results yet.
- **AI as Coordination-Compressing Capital: Task Reallocation, Organizational Redesign, and the Regime Fork** (Farach, arXiv:2602.16078) — a purely theoretical model of AI-driven organizational redesign; no empirical validation. Previously excluded from several earlier digests.
- **Economic Governance of Autonomous Agents and Robots** (Syed et al., arXiv:2609.32476) — a fiscal-policy design proposal illustrated on a hypothetical logistics setting; no empirical data or market outcome.
- **Digital Labor Platforms as Infrastructure of Extractivism** (Posada, arXiv:2609.37614) — a critical-theory book chapter on AI data-annotation labor; qualitative/conceptual, no quantified labor-market design.
- **Skill, Luck, or Imitation? What Actually Pays in Creator Markets** (Tariq, arXiv:2609.32925) — a rich empirical study of creator-economy earnings; no AI or automation mechanism.
- **Measuring the prevalence and direction of innovation: a pilot approach using LLMs** (Garg, Lambert, Martin & Medvedev; CEP DP 2216 / CAGE WP 825) — a strong firm-level design (LLM-coded innovation survey across ~600,000 firms) that would be directly on-topic, but both RePEc and the NEP-LMA listing still give only "2026" with no month or day, so window eligibility cannot be confirmed without guessing.
- **The Early Impacts of AI on Employment among Recent College Graduates** (Fairlie & Wu; NBER WP 35796 / IZA DP 18945) — a causal CPS-based design directly on topic, but both NBER and IZA still give only "September 2026" with no day; excluded for date uncertainty, as in several earlier digests.

## Sourcing note
