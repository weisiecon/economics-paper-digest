# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-28

## 1. Organizing Intelligence [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Alex Imas, Andrew Koh, Shane Legg
**Date:** first posted 2026-10-31
**Source:** arXiv:2611.05417
**Summary:** The authors build an equilibrium model in which human and AI agents with multidimensional capabilities sort endogenously across competing firms, separating a firm's "competence" (which sets productivity levels) from its "coordination" capacity (which sets how productivity scales with firm size), and use it to trace how market structure, employment, and wages evolve as AI capability improves. They find that while AI remains below humans' coordination limits, teams of complementary humans can still outperform AI and even gain from it, but once AI's own coordination ability improves enough to break the ceiling on firm size, the market can tip suddenly toward a few large, AI-heavy, highly concentrated firms; wage changes decompose into a technology effect (how AI changes the value of incumbent humans) and a reorganization effect (how firms resize and recompose), and the latter can reverse the former. The model offers a structural reason why AI's effects on employment and wages could look gradual for an extended period and then flip abruptly past a coordination threshold — a distinction useful for interpreting the currently mixed empirical record on AI and labor markets.
