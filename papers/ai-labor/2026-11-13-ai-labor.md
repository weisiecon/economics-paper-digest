# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-13

No papers qualified for inclusion today: the arXiv harvest and supplemental searches turned up candidates that were either already logged in a prior digest, lacked a genuine AI linkage, used AI only as a measurement instrument rather than as the subject of study, or could not be pinned to a confirmed date inside the 30-day window.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Human Capital in Context: What Employment Survival Reveals About Worker-Firm Match** (arXiv 2611.16058) — uses a two-tower neural network to estimate worker-firm match quality from employment survival, grounded in the skill-weights model of firm-specific human capital; a real labor-economics measure, but AI is used only as the estimation tool, not as the subject of study.
- **Labor Market Returns to Education and Intra-family Educational Inequality: Theory and Experimental Evidence** (arXiv 2611.02959) — a well-identified theory-plus-lab-in-the-field paper on wage returns to education and sibling inequality, but no AI linkage.
- **A Framework for Task-Agnostic Counterfactual Fairness in Transformers through Activation Steering** (arXiv 2611.00298) — a mechanistic-interpretability bias-mitigation method that cites hiring and lending only as example application domains, with no empirical hiring-outcome data.
- **The GenAI Who Knew Too Little: Revisiting Transactive Memory Systems in Human GenAI Collaboration** (arXiv 2610.28343) — a qualitative interview study of 14 knowledge workers on human-GenAI collaboration; not an economics-style quantitative design.
- **Beyond Exposure: AI and Labor Market Reorganization in Türkiye** (Nxumalo & Raju, IZA DP 19026) — a strong, directly on-topic finding (microdata-based AI Jobs Transition Framework applied to Türkiye's 2024 labor force survey, decomposing exposed employment into Automation Risk/Reorganize/Grow-with-AI/Less-Change shares). The paper's own cover page gives only "October 2026," with no day; unable to confirm whether it falls before or after the October 14 window cutoff without exceeding the fetch budget, so excluded rather than guessed. Worth re-checking in a future digest once a precise date is available.
- **Game theory of academic effort and hiring in the age of AI** (arXiv 2610.35802) — already logged in the 2026-10-28 ai-science digest; purely theoretical (no empirical data) and specific to academic hiring, so its labor-market contribution is not distinct enough to warrant a separate entry here.

The arXiv harvest API succeeded on the first attempt (2000 entries returned, 1132 within the 30-day window, 70 passing the keyword filter, 43 surviving dedupe against prior digests), but none cleared the relevance bar. The supplemental web search budget (4 searches, 4 fetches) surfaced several genuinely on-topic IZA/CEPR AI-labor papers, but all were dated outside the 30-day window (IZA DP 18945, DP 18740, DP 18751, DP 18521 — June-September 2026) or, in the one promising case (IZA DP 19026), had only a month-level publication date with no confirmable day.
