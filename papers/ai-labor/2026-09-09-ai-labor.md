## 1. Bias in the Tails: How Name-conditioned Evaluative Framing in Resume Summaries Destabilizes LLM-based Hiring [Hiring]

**Authors:** Huy Nghiem, Phuong-Anh Nguyen-Le, Sy-Tuyen Ho, Hal Daume
**Date:** Submitted 2026-04-21; revised 2026-09-07
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19984
**Summary:** Nghiem et al. audit four LLMs used to write hiring-relevant candidate summaries, generating nearly one million summaries under systematic race- and gender-coded name perturbations applied to synthetic resumes matched against real job postings. Separating each summary's factual resume content from the model's evaluative framing, they find the facts stay essentially stable while the evaluative language shifts with a candidate's name, with the effect concentrated in the tails of the distribution and strongest in open-source models. A hiring simulation shows this turns what looks like a directional bias into a form of symmetric instability that conventional fairness audits are likely to miss, and that chaining LLMs together (one model's summary feeding another's decision) risks compounding the problem.
