# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-23

## 1. The Rise of Agents and the Human Labor Market [Employment]

**Authors:** Nicola Borri, Anton Cheremukhin, Yukun Liu, Aleh Tsyvinski
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-20
**Source:** arXiv:2611.27210
**Summary:** The authors build a firm-level measure of "agentic beta" — exposure to aggregate agentic AI token consumption — by combining proprietary OpenRouter data on roughly two quadrillion realized AI tokens (including 1.1 quadrillion agentic tokens) across 400+ models with the comovement of U.S. public firms' stock returns to that consumption, then match it to 34.8 million U.S. job postings from Lightcast. They find that a one-standard-deviation increase in a firm's agentic AI exposure predicts 2.11% lower job-posting growth over six months and 3.20% over nine months, with an event study around the Claude Code 2.0 release showing a stronger effect (4.73% over six months) concentrated entirely in non-senior roles; broad (non-agentic) AI exposure shows no comparable relationship once agentic exposure is controlled for. The paper is the first economy-wide, cross-model evidence that it is specifically agentic AI capability — not generative AI exposure in general — that is already depressing labor demand for junior positions at exposed firms.
