# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-09-23

No paper met the relevance bar today. The arXiv harvest (econ.GN, econ.EM, cs.CY) returned 2,000 entries (API cap reached), of which 999 fell within the 30-day window; 59 matched the labor-market keyword filter, 47 survived dedupe against prior digests (12 were already covered, 0 overlapped with `ai-science`), and none combined clear AI relevance with a research design an economist would recognize (causal identification, field/natural experiment, firm- or market-level outcome, or welfare analysis). NBER RSS supplementation was unavailable (see Unreachable sources); web-search supplementation surfaced only AI-labor papers that fall outside the 30-day window (see Excluded, borderline).

## Excluded, borderline

- **''Bless his heart... he thought all we did was push a button": Understanding Worker Challenges with U.S. Election Technology** (arXiv:2609.19233) — a qualitative interview/survey study of election officials' difficulties with voting technology; no AI-specific treatment and no causal or market-outcome design.
- **An evidence review of worker retraining** (arXiv:2609.07011) — an exhaustive meta-analysis of 56 RCTs of job-training programs since 1973; strong evidence synthesis, but the treatment is subsidized job training generally, not AI.
- **Detecting Deceptive Recruitment** (arXiv:2609.20336) — a signal-theoretic ML detection framework for fraudulent job ads; benchmark-style system paper, no labor-market outcome finding.
- **Mitigating Fabrication in Multi-Stage LLM Pipelines for Hiring** (arXiv:2608.26171, revised 2026-09-16) — a controlled evaluation of prompt-guardrail and human-in-the-loop mitigations for fabricated content in AI hiring pipelines; measures output fabrication rates, not a worker or labor-market outcome.
- **Prepared Or Unprepared? Healthcare Workforce Readiness in Nigeria** (arXiv:2609.19096) — descriptive attitudes/readiness survey of clinicians; no causal or market-outcome design.
- **AI-Driven Feedback Systems, Digital Labour, and Silent Quitting** (arXiv:2609.16192) — descriptive/conceptual treatment of AI workplace monitoring and disengagement; no empirical data.
- **When Does AI Augment Work?** (arXiv:2609.12482) — a definitional human-agent collaboration framework with an illustrative case study; no empirical worker-outcome design.
- **Mindspeller Neuroprofiling** (arXiv:2609.12501) — a pilot EEG/task-performance tool for O*NET-based occupational matching; a psychometric instrument validation, not a labor-market research design.
- **From Network Inequality to Network Fairness** (arXiv:2609.13867) — a conceptual fairness framework using academic hiring only as an illustrative example.
- **AI and the Economy: An Economic Examination...** (arXiv:2609.01263) — a broad review essay on AI's economic channels; no empirical identification strategy.
- **Agentic Inequality** (arXiv:2510.16853, revised 2026-09-05) — a conceptual essay on inequality in AI-agent access; no empirical labor-market design.
- **Digital Engagement, Income Disparities, and Job Seeking in the US since 2010** (arXiv:2511.05294, revised 2026-08-27) — a strong quasi-causal design (NLSY97, doubly robust transition estimates), but the treatment is general internet-use frequency, not AI specifically.
- **Beyond Exposure: Predicting AI Adoption Based on Comparative Advantage** (Lindenlaub, Oh, Rodriguez & Veldkamp, NBER w35271, May 2026) — a comparative-advantage index that explains far more adoption variance than exposure measures alone; strong design, but posted outside the 30-day window.
- **AI, Task Changes in Jobs, and Worker Reallocation** (Gathmann, Grimm & Winkler, IZA DP 17554, Dec 2024) — patent/robot/AI exposure design showing task reallocation and divergent wage effects by skill; posted well outside the 30-day window.
