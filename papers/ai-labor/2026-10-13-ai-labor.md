# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-10-13

## 1. AI-based matching improves refugee employment in a double-blind randomized trial [Employment]

**Authors:** Kirk Bansak, Jens Hainmueller, Dominik Hangartner, Jeremy Ferwerda, Elisabeth Paulson, Angie Delevoye, Nicholas Adams-Cohen, Ashwin Ramaswami, Selina Kurer, Joelle Pianzola, Michael Hotard
**Date:** Submitted 2026-09-28
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35448
**Summary:** Between 2020 and 2023, Switzerland's State Secretariat for Migration randomly assigned roughly 2,000 refugee resettlement cases to receive either an algorithmically optimized canton recommendation, built from administrative data and machine learning to predict where each case would find work, or an assignment drawn to mimic existing placement procedures, with officers and refugees blind to which arm a case was in. Employment rose by about 2.2 percentage points over three years in the pooled sample and by nearly 4 points in the post-pandemic cohorts, with the effect still growing at the 36-month mark, roughly comparable to the gains from hundreds of hours of language training. Because the trial held canton-level capacity quotas fixed across arms, the result isolates the value of better matching refugees to local labor markets rather than simply steering them toward already-strong economies, offering rare field evidence that algorithmic decision support can meaningfully improve a high-stakes public allocation problem.

## 2. ABLEIST: Intersectional Disability Bias in LLM-Generated Hiring Scenarios [Hiring]

**Authors:** Mahika Phutane, Hayoung Jung, Matthew Kim, Tanushree Mitra, Aditya Vashistha
**Date:** first posted 2025-10-13; revised 2026-10-08
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10998
**Summary:** Phutane, Jung, Kim, Mitra, and Vashistha audit six large language models across 2,820 simulated hiring scenarios that vary disability type alongside gender, nationality, and caste, focusing on candidates from the Global South who are largely absent from existing bias research. They introduce ABLEIST, a set of ableism-specific and intersectional harm metrics covering inspiration-based framing, superhumanization, and tokenism, and find that disabled candidates face significantly elevated harms that most of the audited models fail to flag as problematic, with the harms compounding sharply for candidates who are simultaneously disabled and marginalized by gender or caste. The results show that current LLM safety tooling, built largely around Western disability framings, misses a specific and intersectional class of hiring harm that could disadvantage disabled jobseekers in the Global South as AI hiring tools spread.

## 3. Chaining Tasks, Redefining Work: A Theory of AI Automation [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Mert Demirer, John J. Horton, Nicole Immorlica, Brendan Lucier, Peyman Shahidi
**Date:** first posted 2026-06-14; revised 2026-10-02
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15960
**Summary:** Demirer, Horton, Immorlica, Lucier, and Shahidi model production as a sequence of interdependent steps that a firm can assign to human labor, AI-augmented labor, or a contiguous block of AI-executed "chains," and solve for the firm's cost-minimizing assignment. They show chaining can override ordinary comparative-advantage logic, since steps where humans are relatively better can still get folded into an AI chain for the sake of continuity, and that the payoff from improving AI quality can rise non-monotonically as exposed steps cluster together. Using data on real automation adoption, they find that steps are more likely to be AI-executed when their workflow neighbors already are, supporting the model's prediction that automation spreads through contiguous clusters of tasks rather than independently across them.

## 4. Can Labor Markets Function in the Age of AI? The Evaluation Bottleneck in Hiring [Hiring]

**Authors:** Itai Ashlagi, Ramesh Johari, Jon Kleinberg, Anushka Murthy
**Date:** Submitted 2026-09-24
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30058
**Summary:** Ashlagi, Johari, Kleinberg, and Murthy build a model of a hiring market in which AI tools that make it easier to generate polished applications also make those applications less informative, so a rational Bayesian firm leans more on coarse signals like prior experience when deciding whom to screen. They show this hurts "inexperienced-compatible" applicants most, since they have no observable experience to fall back on once individualized material stops being credible, and that when screening is costly the shift can produce outright market failures where firms screen no one or only the already-experienced. The model predicts firms respond by adding a cheap intermediate assessment stage before costly full screening, reframing the central AI-era hiring friction as a shortage of credible evaluation rather than a shortage of applications.

## 5. Complementarity, Augmentation, or Substitutivity? The Impact of Generative Artificial Intelligence on the U.S. Federal Workforce [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** William G. Resh, Yi Ming, Xinyao Xia, Michael Overton, Gul Nisa Gurbuz, Brandon De Bruhl
**Date:** first posted 2025-03-12; revised 2026-10-08
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09637
**Summary:** Resh, Ming, Xia, Overton, Gurbuz, and De Bruhl use a retrieval-augmented large language model system, grounded in curated evidence about each role's actual duties, to score several hundred U.S. federal occupational series on how generative AI affects their knowledge, skills, and abilities along three dimensions: complementing existing competencies, requiring workers to augment their skills, or substituting for human tasks. They find generative AI's near-term impact on the federal workforce is overwhelmingly on the complementarity and augmentation side, and that even the occupations scoring highest on substitutability show only partial automation of routine subtasks rather than wholesale displacement. The authors argue this evidence undercuts workforce-reduction plans premised on AI-driven displacement and instead supports targeted reskilling and competency-based workforce planning.
