# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-15

No papers qualified for inclusion today. The mandated arXiv harvest API (`export.arxiv.org`, `cat:econ.GN|econ.EM|cs.CY` listing query) returned HTTP 429 ("Rate exceeded") on all 10 attempts across roughly 15 minutes of increasing backoff (20s, 45s, and 90s intervals, plus one 503), including after dropping `max_results` to 1000 per the retry instruction; a later targeted `id_list` query to the same API also returned 429, so no fresh arXiv candidate pool could be harvested at all. NBER's RSS feeds remained unreachable (`papers.rss` redirects to a 404 HTML page; `programs/ls/papers.rss` redirects to an HTML landing page, not XML). The supplement tier (4 web searches, 4 page fetches, both at budget) surfaced several genuinely AI-and-labor-market papers via IZA, NBER, CEPR/VoxEU, and RePEc's NEP-AIN report, but every one failed on relevance, duplication, or date confirmation — see below.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Beyond Exposure: AI and Labor Market Reorganization in Türkiye** (Nxumalo & Raju, IZA DP 19026) — strong, directly on-topic microdata finding (AI Jobs Transition Framework applied to Türkiye's 2024 labor force survey), but the IZA page still gives only "October 2026" with no day; re-fetched today and still unable to confirm it falls inside the window without exceeding the fetch budget. Same exclusion as the 2026-11-13 digest.
- **AI Advice Reinforces Traditional Gender Roles** (Born, Curhan & Bowles, arXiv 2610.14036) — a well-identified audit experiment (LLMs give lower salary targets to female personas), but it measures bias in AI-generated advice content, not an actual employment, wage, or hiring outcome; already excluded on identical grounds in the 2026-10-17 ai-econ digest.
- **How Is AI Affecting College Major and Course Choices? Evidence from Finland** (Domnisoru & Heikkinen, CESifo WP 13105) — the same paper already covered as IZA DP 19056 in the 2026-11-06 ai-labor digest, now re-surfaced under a different working-paper series.
- **Displacement Is Endogenous: AI Workers in a Search-and-Matching Labor Market** (Fofanah, arXiv 2610.14850) and **Learning about Markets from Models: Evidence from an AI Hiring Platform** (Alidaee, Khanna & Koning, arXiv 2611.01226) — already logged in the 2026-10-14 and 2026-11-03 ai-labor digests respectively.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Support for Labor Market Policies: Evidence from a Survey Experiment** (Angei, Balia, Brau & Sulis, GLO DP 1856) and **Regulating AI Displacement: Public Demand, Institutional Credibility, and the Politics of Employment Protection in Latin America** (Rosino & Scartascini, IDB) — both survey genuine AI-and-labor-policy preferences, but neither reports a worker, wage, or employment outcome; they are political-economy studies of attitudes toward AI displacement, not of displacement itself.
- **When Everyone Writes Well: The Economics of Writing with AI** (Fourie), **Too Late to Prune: Generative AI, the Screen, and Team Creative Decisions** (Huang, Yu, Chen & Zhou, arXiv 2610.14984), **Who Pays for AI?** (Datta, Lambert & Schneider) — each touches productivity or adoption tangentially but centers on writing quality, team creative process, or consumer adoption rather than worker/labor-market outcomes.
