# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-10-19

## 1. AI-exposed jobs deteriorated before ChatGPT [Employment]

**Authors:** Morgan R. Frank, Alireza Javadian Sabet, Lisa Simon, Sarah H. Bana, Renzhe Yu
**Date:** first posted 2026-01-05; revised 2026-10-16
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02554
**Summary:** Using millions of U.S. unemployment-insurance records, LinkedIn profiles, and university syllabi, the authors track occupation-specific unemployment risk, graduate placement rates into AI-exposed jobs, and the link between LLM-related coursework and job-search outcomes. They find unemployment risk in AI-exposed occupations began rising in early 2022 and graduate cohorts from 2021 onward entered those jobs at lower rates, both months before ChatGPT's late-2022 release, while graduates with more LLM-exposed coursework had shorter job searches and higher first-job pay afterward. The result undercuts the popular "ChatGPT caused AI job losses" narrative, pointing instead to earlier structural forces and to AI-relevant education as a buffer against labor-market disruption.

## 2. Collaborating with AI Agents: Field Experiments on Teamwork, Productivity, and Performance [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Harang Ju, Sinan Aral
**Date:** first posted 2025-03-23; revised 2026-10-15
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18238
**Summary:** The authors randomly assigned 2,234 participants to human-human or human-AI teams producing 11,024 ads, then ran a field experiment on X garnering roughly five million impressions to see how each team type's output performed with real audiences. Human-AI teams produced 50% more ads with higher text quality, which raised click-through rates and view time, while human-human teams produced higher image quality, which improved cost-per-click; AI-driven gains came mainly through delegation and task-oriented communication, which also made outputs more homogeneous. The findings give field-validated evidence that AI's productivity gains for workers are real but uneven across skill types and hinge on how teams organize collaboration, posing an explicit productivity-versus-diversity tradeoff for firms redesigning work around AI agents.

## 3. Task Expansion with Generative AI: Experimental Evidence on Middle-Skilled, Early-Career Workers [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Hanna Brosch, Christina Langer, Philipp Lergetporer, Sandra Mueller, Harald Pfeifer, Nele Tschoepe
**Date:** first posted October 2026 (day not stated in source)
**Source:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/18999
**Summary:** In a field experiment, 673 final-year IT apprentices at twelve German vocational schools were randomly given access to generative AI for occupation-specific tasks inside and outside their formal curriculum. AI raised productivity on every task type, with the largest gains (26-31 percentage points) on tasks the apprentices had not been trained for, without costing them task comprehension, and effects were larger for apprentices with above-median AI literacy. The design offers clean causal evidence that generative AI can let early-career, middle-skilled workers take on tasks beyond their formal training, suggesting AI may expand entry-level workers' task scope rather than simply substitute for their existing skills.
