# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-21

## 1. AI-Induced Social Erosion: Using LLMs for Work Tasks Reduces Help-Seeking Intentions Towards Colleagues [Firm Adoption]

**Authors:** Kevin Chow, Sophia Deng, Bowen He, Hanyi Min, Qiaosi Wang, Koustuv Saha, Vedant Das Swain
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-18
**Source:** arXiv:2611.25269
**Summary:** The authors ran an online experimental vignette study in which 248 knowledge workers evaluated scenarios that varied whether and how generative AI was used across different work tasks, then used linear mixed-effects models to estimate how AI use shapes intentions to seek help from colleagues. AI-enabled work reduced help-seeking intentions relative to non-AI work, with the effect strongest for brainstorming tasks, while visibly disclosed AI use partly offset the drop and individual contributors' colleague relationships proved more vulnerable than managers'. The experimental design isolates a channel by which routine AI adoption inside firms could erode the informal ties that coworker networks and on-the-job learning depend on, independent of any effect on output or headcount.

## 2. AI-Driven Transformation of Digital Platform Work: A Task-Based Model for Measuring Labour Disruption and Vulnerability, with Early Evidence from Online Freelancing in Kenya [Employment]

**Authors:** Farhan Latif
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-19
**Source:** arXiv:2611.25585
**Summary:** The author builds a task-based framework that separates AI-driven "disruption" (how much work changes) from "livelihood vulnerability" (how exposed a worker's income is to that change), and applies it to four categories of online platform work — writing, virtual assistance, transcription, and web and software development — before checking the model's predictions against a mixed-methods survey of 52 online freelancers and 20 follow-up interviews in Nairobi, Kenya. The model finds high disruption across all four task categories but lower and more unevenly distributed vulnerability, with potential efficiency gains on unpaid work partly offsetting wage losses on paid tasks; interviewed workers confirm changed task performance and intensifying competition but describe adapting skills and using AI strategically in ways the model does not fully anticipate. The gap between the model's projections and freelancers' lived experience illustrates why occupation-level exposure measures can mislead about actual displacement risk in platform labor markets.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Decent Work Standards and Non-Conventional Work... Kenya's Online Platform Labour Markets** (Latif, arXiv:2611.23757) — a companion study of the same freelancer population, but it assesses general decent-work conditions and capability formation with no AI-specific content; excluded as off-topic for an AI & labor digest.
- **HireWatch: Evaluating LLM Compliance with U.S. Employment Law Under Contextual Pressure** (Nghiem, Kostylew & Kolt, arXiv:2611.24970) — audits whether LLMs select unlawful interview questions under prompt pressure; measures model behavior in a constructed test bank, not any actual hiring or labor-market outcome (same basis on which a prior LLM-hiring-audit paper was excluded on 2026-11-17).
- **Human Capital in Context: What Employment Survival Reveals About Worker-Firm Match** (Choi & Milone, arXiv:2611.16058) — a worker-firm match-quality measure built with a neural-network embedding method, but the paper is about match quality and separations generally, with no AI-exposure or AI-adoption content.
- **Good Looks and the Academic Job Market: Measuring Attractiveness with Vision Embeddings** (Slonimczyk & Kimsanbaev, arXiv:2611.10447) — uses a vision model only as a measurement instrument for an appearance-bias-in-hiring study; not about AI's effect on labor demand or work.
- **Reconstructing Subnational Labor Indicators in Colombia** (Vera-Jaramillo, arXiv:2508.12514v3) — a machine-learning method for reconstructing official labor statistics, not a study of AI's labor-market impact.
- **Beyond Exposure: AI and Labor Market Reorganization in Türkiye** (Nxumalo & Raju, IZA DP No. 19026) — relevant in scope (reclassifies occupations by AI exposure, demand responsiveness, and "human necessity" using Türkiye's 2024 labor force survey) but excluded on dating grounds: IZA and RePEc list only "October 2026," with no day, so it cannot be confirmed to fall inside the 30-day window ending 2026-11-21.
- **Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a Randomized Experiment** (Cruces, Fernández Meijide, Galiani, Gálvez & Lombardi, CEPR DP21299) — a strong RCT design (closes ~75% of an education-based performance gap under AI access), but its NEP announcement dates to July 2026, well outside the 30-day window; excluded as out of window, not fetched further.
