# AI & Labor Markets Papers — 2026-11-20

## 1. AI-Driven Transformation of Digital Platform Work: A Task-Based Model for Measuring Labour Disruption and Vulnerability, with Early Evidence from Online Freelancing in Kenya [Employment]

**Authors:** Farhan Latif
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-19
**Source:** arXiv:2611.25585
**Summary:** The author builds a task-based framework that separates "AI-driven disruption" (how much a task's performance changes) from "livelihood vulnerability" (whether that change actually threatens a worker's income), rather than relying on standard occupation-level exposure scores, and applies it to four categories of online freelance work: writing, virtual assistance, transcription, and web/software development. The model predicts high disruption across all four categories but considerably lower and more occupation-specific vulnerability, partly because AI-driven efficiency gains on unpaid tasks offset some of the wage losses from paid work; a survey and interviews with 52 Nairobi-based freelancers broadly support this picture, with workers reporting changed task methods, intensifying competition, and strategic use of AI to stay employable. The contribution is descriptive rather than causal, but it offers a task-level alternative to occupational exposure indices and highlights a gap between the model's projections and freelancers' own sense of job-loss risk.

## 2. AI-Induced Social Erosion: Using LLMs for Work Tasks Reduces Help-Seeking Intentions Towards Colleagues [Skills & Tasks]

**Authors:** Kevin Chow, Sophia Deng, Bowen He, Hanyi Min, Qiaosi Wang, Koustuv Saha, Vedant Das Swain
**Date:** first posted 2026-11-18
**Source:** arXiv:2611.25269
**Summary:** The authors ran an online vignette experiment with 248 knowledge workers who rated hypothetical scenarios that varied whether and how a colleague used generative AI to complete a work task, then used linear mixed-effects models to link AI use to workers' stated intentions to seek help from coworkers. Using AI on a task reduced help-seeking intentions relative to doing the same task without AI, with the effect strongest for brainstorming; the drop was smaller when the AI use was visible to others, and individual contributors' colleague relationships appeared more vulnerable to the effect than managers'. The findings point to a hidden organizational cost of generative-AI adoption — erosion of the everyday interactions that sustain workplace relationships and knowledge-sharing — that the authors argue should inform how firms design AI tools and usage norms, not just how they measure productivity gains.

## Excluded, borderline

- **Reconstructing Subnational Labor Indicators in Colombia: An Integrated Machine and Deep Learning Approach** (Vera-Jaramillo, arXiv:2508.12514, revised 2026-11-19) — uses ML/deep learning purely as a statistical-reconstruction tool to interpolate official labor indicators; it is not about AI's effect on workers or the labor market.
- **Human Capital in Context: What Employment Survival Reveals About Worker-Firm Match** (Choi & Milone, arXiv:2611.16058) — a strong empirical paper on worker-firm match quality and its effects on wages, separations, and firm growth, but the neural-network embedding is a measurement method for a classic human-capital question, not an AI-adoption or AI-exposure treatment.
- **Good Looks and the Academic Job Market: Measuring Attractiveness with Vision Embeddings** (Slonimczyk & Kimsanbaev, arXiv:2611.10447) — studies how physical attractiveness affects academic hiring outcomes; vision embeddings are used only to construct the attractiveness measure, with no AI-labor nexus.
- **HireWatch: Evaluating LLM Compliance with U.S. Employment Law Under Contextual Pressure** (Nghiem, Kostylew & Kolt, arXiv:2611.24970) — audits whether LLMs select lawful interview questions under pressure; it measures model compliance behavior, not an actual hiring or worker outcome.
- **A Taxonomy-Driven Synthetic Dataset for Retrieval Effectiveness and Fairness Evaluation in Hiring** (Jouanneau et al., arXiv:2611.09178) — a synthetic benchmark for auditing HR-embedding models, not a finding about real worker or labor-market outcomes.
- A VoxEU column reporting that Danish firms adopting AI hire fewer new employees than non-adopters, built on Danmarks Nationalbank Working Paper No. 223 (Bonin, Darougheh & Kuchler) — a genuine firm-level natural-experiment design, but the underlying paper is dated 2026-09-16, well outside the 30-day window, and the column itself could not be opened (HTTP 403) to confirm a later publication date.
